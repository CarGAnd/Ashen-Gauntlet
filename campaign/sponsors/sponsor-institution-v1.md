# Sponsor Institution V1

Status: NPC Designer working canon proposal. Define and approve this institution before creating broad sponsor rosters or sponsor mechanics.

## Core definition

A **Sponsor** is a former Ashen Gauntlet Victor, or occasionally another specially approved veteran of comparable relevance, assigned by the Games to one registered contestant team.

Sponsors prepare contestants for the realities, customs, dangers, and institutional expectations of the Gauntlet; escort them through the pre-Games process; and personally deliver them to the final insertion handoff. Their task is to prepare judgment, not to make decisions for the team.

Once insertion occurs, the Sponsor's active role ends. They cannot communicate with the contestants, send advice, deliver equipment, alter their route, or intervene on their behalf. The traditional formal reunion occurs only if that team later qualifies as a Victor.

Most Sponsors are former Victors. Former Prime Victors are rare Sponsors because there are very few of them and their political/cultural weight can overwhelm an assignment.## Who may serve

The normal qualification is **Victor status**: the person survived a previous Games as part of a team that completed a five-color set and qualified at the Crucible.

A non-Victor may serve only by explicit approval of the Council's civil Games administration and must possess unusually relevant experience: for example a celebrated expedition leader, former contestant who reached the final threshold without qualifying, retired Games official with field experience, or equivalent veteran. These exceptions should remain uncommon enough that "my Sponsor was a Victor" is the normal expectation.

Prime Victors are eligible but seldom assigned. Their fame, political commitments, and symbolic power make ordinary mentorship difficult, and several hold offices more influential than Sponsorship.

A Sponsor need not resemble the team's classes, species, tactics, homeland, or preferred route. The institution explicitly rejects the idea that every team should receive an optimized coach.

## Is service compulsory?

Every registered team receives a Sponsor assignment. Contestants may dislike, ignore, or argue with that Sponsor, but they do not bypass the institutional process.

Eligible Sponsors are maintained on a **Roll of Return**. Victor status carries an expectation of future service while the Victor remains capable, but no Victor is automatically compelled to serve every Games.

A Sponsor may decline or recuse from a proposed assignment for health, office, oath, personal conflict, prior relationship, credible bias, or other serious reason. Repeated refusal without accepted cause can suspend some ceremonial and archival privileges for that Games cycle, but does not erase Victor status.

This makes Sponsorship a meaningful obligation attached to prestige without turning every former Victor into a permanent employee.## How Sponsor-team pairing works

Pairing is a mixture of deliberate judgment, controlled chance, and politics.

1. **Conflict screening.** The Games remove Sponsors with direct family ties, active debts, command relationships, hostile contracts, unresolved litigation, or other conflicts likely to compromise the assignment.
2. **Deliberate shortlist.** The Sponsor office identifies several people whose temperament, lived Gauntlet experience, cultural fluency, or ability to challenge the team's assumptions could be useful. This is not class matching.
3. **Controlled draw.** The final assignment is selected from that viable shortlist rather than hand-optimized. This preserves uncertainty and limits accusations that the Games secretly engineered perfect pairings.
4. **Political pressure exists.** Foreign rulers, temples, guilds, organisers, and famous contestants can lobby for or against particular pairings. Such pressure can change the shortlist, especially for politically important teams, but it does not normally determine every assignment.

The routine pairing process is administered by the Games' Sponsor office under the Council of the Crucible. Nadira's institutional sphere shapes contracts and conflicts of interest; Metonius' security apparatus vets dangerous connections; the Eight do not personally assign all twenty-five teams.

Contestants may file a preference or request a specific eligible Sponsor. The request carries no entitlement. A requested Sponsor must be eligible, willing, and approved. Contestants may request reassignment for a genuine conflict, not merely because they dislike the Sponsor's advice or personality.

## What authority a Sponsor has

A Sponsor is not the team's commander, owner, financier, or legal guardian.

They may instruct during formal preparation, inspect readiness, explain rules and traditions, challenge assumptions, recommend equipment changes, arrange approved training, and advise withdrawal before insertion. They cannot force tactical obedience, alter the team's registration, choose its insertion location or gem, or withdraw a competent adult contestant against that contestant's will.

Their strongest authority is procedural: they know the institution, can get the team into the correct rooms, can demand that officials answer questions contestants are entitled to ask, and can certify that the required briefing and handoff occurred.## What Sponsors may tell contestants

Sponsors may freely teach:
- the public rules and victory conditions;
- the normal meaning of gems, containers, qualification, Favor, Spotlight, surrender, and the Closing Ring;
- their own remembered experience of a previous Games;
- public history of previous contestants and Victors;
- broad truths about the volcano's danger, cultures, route logic, resource pressure, and the fact that old maps become unreliable;
- practical advice on negotiation, camps, injury, pursuit, rest, deception, gem security, and when to abandon a bad plan;
- their personal reading of the Eight, provided they distinguish opinion from rule.

Sponsors are not granted a complete current-run map. They are deliberately excluded from exact current insertion assignments, starting gem colors, sealed security information, current rival intelligence, hidden organiser decisions, and other run-specific material that would turn mentorship into advance reconnaissance.

A Sponsor may recount routes or secrets personally discovered in an earlier Games unless those details remain protected by a specific security oath. Such information is never guaranteed to remain accurate. The mountain changes, inhabitants move, structures collapse, and the Games themselves alter access.

Sponsors are bound by a formal confidentiality oath covering restricted current-run information and protected Games infrastructure. Breach can cost Sponsor standing, archive access, future assignments, and in severe cases other Victor privileges. The deeper practical safeguard is compartmentalization: Sponsors cannot leak what they were never told.

## What assistance they may secure before insertion

Sponsors may help contestants obtain approved training space, equipment inspection, replacement mundane gear, legal advice, interpreters, medical attention, introductions to legitimate vendors, public archives, and other preparation resources available through the Games.

They may recommend how a team spends its own preparation resources and may make declared personal gifts within Games scrutiny. Sponsorship is not a loophole for transferring legendary artifacts, secret maps, armies of retainers, or unlimited private wealth into the arena.

Exact equipment ceilings and preparation budgets, if needed, belong to later Systems/Session design. The NPC rule is simple: a Sponsor can improve preparation and access, but cannot purchase victory.## The insertion boundary

The Sponsor accompanies the team through registration, medical and legal processing, equipment checks, ceremonies, final briefing, and the physical journey to the sealed insertion handoff.

At that boundary, the relationship changes completely.

After insertion:
- no messages;
- no magical sending;
- no item drops;
- no tactical updates;
- no route warnings;
- no Sponsor-arranged rescue;
- no use of organiser observation magic to speak back into the arena.

Sponsors may watch the Games through the same legal audience/official systems available to their station, but observation is one-way. A Sponsor who discovers that their team is walking toward catastrophe cannot warn them.

The formal Sponsor assignment is considered fulfilled at handoff. If the team dies, withdraws through whatever legal mechanisms may exist, or simply never qualifies, there is no required in-Games reunion. If the team becomes a Victor, tradition gives the Sponsor a place among the first non-organisers permitted to receive them after qualification.

That reunion is the payoff of the institution: the person who delivered them into the Gauntlet is present when they return from it as a Victor.

## What the institution expects from Sponsors

A Sponsor is expected to give contestants a truthful understanding of the Games without pretending to know their future.

They are expected to prepare the team psychologically as well as tactically: other contestants are not encounter packets, old information may be false, retreat can be correct, apparent allies may have incompatible objectives, injuries compound, gems create incentives, and there is no promise that a fair fight will occur.

They are also expected to preserve institutional legitimacy. Deliberate sabotage, corrupt gifts, current-run intelligence leaks, secret coordination between assigned teams, or using Sponsorship to settle an outside feud are serious violations.

Sponsors are not required to like their contestants. They are required to deliver them honestly prepared.## When a Sponsor opposes an organiser

Sponsors may argue, petition, appeal a procedural ruling, criticize an organiser publicly, or refuse to endorse a recommendation before insertion. Former Victors often possess enough prestige to make such disagreement politically visible.

They cannot countermand an organiser's lawful authority, alter arena conditions, or carry a private dispute into the inserted team's support. A serious confrontation can result in the Sponsor being reassigned, censured, losing access, or having their complaint escalated to the wider Council rather than the individual organiser.

The institution tolerates disagreement because credible former Victors are part of its legitimacy. It does not tolerate a Sponsor using contestants as proxies in a personal war with the Eight.

## Payment and motive

Sponsors receive travel, lodging, staff support, preparation expenses, and a respectable ceremonial honorarium. Sponsorship is prestigious work, but the normal payment is not large enough to explain why already-famous Victors return.

Former Victors return for many reasons: duty, tradition, nostalgia, guilt, prestige, political access, genuine mentorship, rivalry with other Victors, desire to shape how the Games are understood, or because Sponsor service keeps them connected to the institution that made their name.

Some love returning. Some consider it a debt. Some hate the ceremonies and still accept the assignment.

## Victor privileges and obligations

Victor status is lifelong unless stripped for extraordinary misconduct. It grants formal recognition within the Games, ceremonial standing, access to the Hall of Victors and selected historical archives, invitations to major Gauntlet functions, and the right to be considered for Sponsor service and other institutional roles.

Victors are expected to submit an official post-Games account, preserve protected security information, refrain from selling restricted current-run intelligence, and remain eligible for reasonable service to the institution while capable.

These privileges do not make every Victor rich, politically powerful, or morally loyal to the Games. They do make Victor status a durable social identity.

Prime Victor status adds exceptional prestige but does not create a separate Sponsor class. A former Prime Victor serving as Sponsor should feel unusual and politically significant.
