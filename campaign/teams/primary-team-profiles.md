# Primary Contestant Team Profiles

Status: Canonical primary-rival character roster. Mechanical identities, team names, contestant identities, personalities, behavioral tendencies, gem responses, ethics, and vulnerabilities are approved canon. Outcomes remain emergent; homeland geography remains intentionally lightweight and provisional.

## Design rule

Nothing in this document predetermines alliances, betrayals, deaths, victories, or encounters with the player party. Behavioral notes describe pressures and decision tendencies only.

Exact insertion anchors and starting gem colors remain separate responsibilities. Sponsor identities are deliberately not assigned yet; each profile records only what kind of sponsor relationship would be productive.

Class labels remain authoring shorthand. Final Foundry combat actors should normally use streamlined class-inspired NPC stat blocks rather than full PC sheets.

## Setting-dependence rule

The Ashen Gauntlet currently has no declared published-world setting. Do not assume Forgotten Realms geography. Homeland names in this file are working original-world geography: the cultural function is more important than the proper noun and may later be revised or mapped if the campaign setting is explicitly chosen.

## A. The Last Standard

**Member:** Kharza Venn — dragonborn, from the Ashkar Reach, a dragonborn-dominated frontier state with a severe legionary culture, approximately 46; level-12 Fighter/Barbarian-style veteran and sole contestant.
**Appearance:** Tall even for a dragonborn, dark bronze scales worn pale around old scars, one horn broken short, controlled posture rather than theatrical bulk.
**Equipment identity:** Blackened half-plate, heavy field cloak, climbing kit, and a long war-spear carrying a narrow weathered campaign pennant. Her gear looks maintained for marching, not display.
**Why she entered:** After decades as a field commander, peace and politics pushed her toward ceremonial work while younger officers began treating her victories as history rather than proof. She entered because she wants the world to know, publicly and without qualification, that she is still one of its greatest living warriors. Her homeland also expects an Ashkar champion to be feared rather than merely respected; failure would not ruin her, but it would humiliate her in exactly the arena she chose to make definitive.
**How the team knows one another / leader:** Not applicable; she deliberately registered alone and regards dependence on hired companions as defeating the purpose of her entry.
**Behavior under fear:** Becomes quieter and more procedural. She shortens objectives, secures defensible ground, and tries to convert uncertainty into a physical problem she can control.
**Killing contestants:** Accepts contestant death without sentimentality. Anyone still capable of contesting her claim, pursuing her later, or recovering to threaten her is a legitimate target. She usually honors a clear surrender because forcing submission proves dominance more cleanly than execution, but mercy is never automatic and repeated enemies may not receive a second offer.
**Bargaining:** Prefers explicit exchanges with few clauses. Once she gives her word, she is difficult to move from it.
**If she loses a gem:** Immediate pursuit if the loss is recent and recoverable; otherwise she suppresses the insult and hunts for another source. Public humiliation makes the decision less rational.
**If she gains several gems:** Treats them as strategic burdens as much as prizes. She keeps them physically close, seeks efficient trades, and becomes less willing to enter encounters without a clear purpose.
**Attitude toward weaker teams:** Sees weakness as a condition, not a moral protection. She will not torment weaker teams for entertainment, but she is entirely willing to demand gems, information, passage, or supplies from them if they cannot credibly refuse. Courage under pressure can earn better terms; helplessness by itself does not.
**Route tendencies:** Broad physical routes, engineered passages, climbs, harsh marches, and obstacles that can be forced or endured. She dislikes delicate magical puzzles and routes requiring several simultaneous actors.
**Sponsor profile requirement:** A military, veteran, or arms patron who supplies durable practical support and respects operational independence. A sponsor trying to issue battlefield orders would quickly become a liability.
**Natural organiser fit:** Arcos is her clearest natural admirer, and Valerius is easy to please when her pride becomes public performance. Metonius respects her competence but may dislike status-driven decisions. Veyra is a frequent point of friction; Kessa approves her endurance but dislikes risking the campaign to answer an insult.
**Memorable voice/behavior:** Speaks in short declarative sentences and unconsciously assigns tactical roles to people around her. Before committing to danger she asks, "What must still be true when this is over?"
**Vulnerabilities:** One-body action economy and magical control; pride becomes exploitable when a public challenge questions her courage or military reputation.

## B. The Glass Star

**Member:** Selyne Qar — human, the Lyris Concord, a sophisticated high-magic republic of academies and observatories, approximately 52; level-11 Wizard-style controller with strong divination and abjuration identity.
**Appearance:** Fine-boned, dark-skinned, close-cropped hair going silver at the temples, usually surrounded by faint geometric motes when concentrating. Dresses for travel rather than court.
**Equipment identity:** Collapsible metal staff, crystal astrolabe focus, layered warding charms, compact spellbook in a brass-edged folio, and several carefully labeled reagent cases.
**Why she entered:** She believes the volcano contains a planar geometry no surviving archive describes correctly. The Games are the only institution that gives her repeated legal access to the mountain, and victory would give her both prestige and leverage to demand deeper archival access afterward.
**How the team knows one another / leader:** Not applicable. She prefers working alone because additional people create variables she cannot fully model.
**Behavior under fear:** Attempts to regain control through analysis. She becomes more likely to expend high-tier magic early, over-ward herself, and narrow her attention to the threat she understands best.
**Killing contestants:** Avoids it when control, escape, or negotiation will do. She is not morally opposed to lethal force and can become frighteningly decisive if she concludes another team presents a persistent future threat.
**Bargaining:** Precise, literal, and excellent at defining terms. She dislikes informal promises because she assumes ambiguity will eventually be weaponized.
**If she loses a gem:** Prefers divination, information purchases, surveillance, and bargaining over physical pursuit. She will abandon a specific gem if retrieving it costs more resources than replacing it.
**If she gains several gems:** Uses duplicates as leverage and begins structuring trades toward a complete set. She also becomes increasingly protective of rest opportunities and spell reserves.
**Attitude toward weaker teams:** Often useful to them and occasionally condescending. She may exchange magical help for information, labor, or future obligations rather than simply taking what she wants.
**Route tendencies:** Strange geometry, sealed passages, vertical bypasses, magical obstacles, and routes other teams consider inaccessible. Strong preference for defensible rest locations.
**Sponsor profile requirement:** A magical academy, research society, collector, or arcane patron willing to provide reagents, scrolls, information, and rest-support rather than demanding public spectacle.
**Natural organiser fit:** Nymor is the clearest match; Metonius and Nadira often appreciate her planning and precise agreements. Arcos and Valerius may find her overly clinical, while Kessa dislikes wasteful spell expenditure when fear overrides discipline.
**Memorable voice/behavior:** Frames uncertainty numerically: "There are three explanations worth respecting." Draws diagrams on any available surface while speaking.
**Vulnerabilities:** Resource and concentration dependence; strong belief that every problem becomes manageable once correctly understood can keep her in situations that should simply be abandoned.

## C. The Black Banner

**Members:** Damas Korr — human, the Keldran Marches, a fortress frontier culture shaped by monster incursions and border wars, approximately 44; level-11 Paladin/Fighter-style defender and close-combat anchor. Saela Thorn — wood elf, raised among the old hunting communities of Elderbough, approximately 128; level-10 Ranger-style scout and ranged striker.
**Appearance:** Damas is broad, greying at the temples, with a badly healed jaw scar and the stillness of a veteran guard captain. Saela is lean, copper-brown, dark-haired, weathered rather than delicate, and almost never stands with her back to an open approach.
**Equipment identity:** Damas carries a dark steel shield and practical longsword beneath scarred plate; Saela carries a recurved longbow, two short blades, field-glass, monster-tracking kits, and a black cloth marker used to designate unsafe ground. Their team name comes from that old quarantine banner.
**Why they entered:** The Keldran Marches sent them as an unofficial demonstration of frontier strength after years of being treated by richer states as a place that merely absorbs monsters for everyone else. Damas wants a victory that cannot be dismissed diplomatically; Saela wants to prove that the methods which keep border settlements alive are superior to courtly heroics. The purse matters, but prestige is the real objective.
**How they know one another:** Eighteen years of joint expeditions and frontier contracts. Their relationship is closer to siblings-in-arms than romance.
**Intra-team relationship:** Damas trusts Saela's judgment before his own when movement or pursuit is involved; Saela trusts Damas to decide when a retreat must become a stand. Both are capable of overriding the other only when they believe the other is acting from fear rather than judgment.
**Leader:** Situational. Damas leads negotiations and close battle; Saela leads travel, scouting, and pursuit.
**Behavior under fear:** Damas becomes more protective and physically interposes himself; Saela increases distance, identifies exits, and begins planning separation-and-regroup tactics.
**Killing contestants:** They are the primary team most comfortable with preventative lethality. Both believe every contestant knowingly entered a contest in which today's spared opponent may kill your partner tomorrow. They will accept surrender when it creates a stable outcome, but if they judge a rival likely to resume the threat, they may kill even after tactical victory rather than gamble on mercy.
**Bargaining:** Strong preference for short, honorable arrangements. They dislike clever wording and will walk away from deals that feel intentionally slippery.
**If they lose a gem:** They determine whether recovery is realistic before acting. Saela will track a thief patiently; Damas will not sacrifice both of them to reclaim one gem from an obviously superior position.
**If they gain several gems:** Become more conservative, trade duplicates if terms are clean, and reduce unnecessary contact. They prefer possession they can defend over hoarding for its own sake.
**Attitude toward weaker teams:** Respect is earned by usefulness, discipline, or courage rather than by vulnerability. They may protect a weaker team that contributes something meaningful, but will also strip strategic resources from a team they judge incapable of defending them. They do not enjoy bullying; they simply reject the idea that weakness creates entitlement.
**Route tendencies:** Dangerous but legible natural or engineered routes, monster territories they can assess, defensible camps, and passages where two elite people can support each other. Avoid unknown one-way mechanisms and multi-person puzzles when alternatives exist.
**Sponsor profile requirement:** A monster-hunting order, border state, veteran foundation, or practical arms patron that values results over performance and does not try to split their command structure.
**Natural organiser fit:** Metonius and Arcos both find much to admire, though for different reasons. Their willingness to eliminate future threats can please Sable when it is strategically clean and strongly irritate Veyra. Kessa respects their fieldcraft; Nadira often dislikes how readily they destroy future bargaining partners.
**Memorable voice/behavior:** Damas asks, "Name the danger," before discussing solutions. Saela habitually counts exits, high ground, and visible hands while other people talk.
**Vulnerabilities:** Their mutual protectiveness can be exploited; separating them sharply reduces their combined threat and makes both more conservative than usual.

## D. Third Bell Company

**Members:** Hadrik Vale — shield dwarf from Durn Kharum, an old northern mining-city, approximately 96, level-8 Fighter-style shield captain. Lessa Fen — lightfoot halfling from the Sapphire Road trade cities, approximately 43, level-8 Rogue-style scout and trap specialist. Corren Hale — human from the Rellin Coast kingdoms, approximately 35, level-7 Cleric-style healer and warder.
**Appearance:** Hadrik is compact, grey-bearded, and missing the tip of one ear; Lessa is wiry, short-haired, and covered in old climbing scars; Corren looks younger than the others but carries the exhausted alertness of someone who has learned not to sleep deeply.
**Equipment identity:** Repaired rather than fashionable gear. Hadrik carries a broad shield with three bell marks, Lessa favors collapsible tools and a shortbow, and Corren carries a travel shrine, healer's satchel, and battered mace. Much of their kit is patterned after what survived their previous Gauntlet.
**Why they entered:** They competed in an earlier Games as a five-person company. Two teammates died and the remaining three survived until that Games concluded without becoming Victors. Hadrik wants to prove their survival was not merely failure; Lessa believes their accumulated knowledge has real value; Corren is the least certain that returning was wise but refused to let the other two go back without him.
**How they know one another:** They were already an adventuring company before their first entry and have years of ordinary contracts plus one Gauntlet behind them.
**Intra-team relationship:** Deep trust complicated by shared loss. Hadrik can become overprotective, Lessa pushes against decisions made from guilt, and Corren often mediates while privately being the most sensitive to signs that history is repeating.
**Leader:** Hadrik is recognized as captain, but route calls require Lessa's agreement and medical withdrawal calls belong to Corren.
**Behavior under fear:** Fall back on rehearsed procedures and terse code words. Separation is the condition most likely to disrupt their judgment.
**Killing contestants:** More realistic about lethality than many first-time teams. They will kill a persistent threat, but avoid unnecessary contestant fights because they know how quickly one injury can become fatal later.
**Bargaining:** Experienced and skeptical. They prefer exchanges that can be completed immediately and dislike debts dependent on everyone surviving for weeks.
**If they lose a gem:** Gather information first. They are unlikely to chase blindly and will seek another copy if recovery requires entering an obviously bad position.
**If they gain several gems:** Their instinct is to stop behaving like hunters and start behaving like qualifiers: reduce exposure, identify missing colors, and trade duplicates rather than collecting indefinitely.
**Attitude toward weaker teams:** Capable of compassion but reluctant to acquire dependents. They may provide directions, healing, or a short escort while refusing an open-ended responsibility for another team's survival.
**Route tendencies:** Routes remembered from the previous Games, defensible fallbacks, known rest opportunities, and infrastructure they understand. Their greatest danger is assuming the mountain still behaves as it did before.
**Sponsor profile requirement:** A patient veteran sponsor willing to trust experienced contestants, finance replacement equipment and intelligence, and tolerate refusal of flashy but strategically poor interventions.
**Natural organiser fit:** Metonius and Kessa strongly; Nadira appreciates practical agreements and Nymor may value their historical route knowledge. Valerius can find their caution unromantic, and Arcos dislikes withdrawals that look driven by old fear rather than current facts.
**Memorable voice/behavior:** They still use bell calls from their old company: "First bell" means move, "second" means prepare to withdraw, "third" means nobody argues until they are safe.
**Vulnerabilities:** Previous-route knowledge can be obsolete; unresolved guilt makes teammate separation, abandonment decisions, and apparently repeat circumstances disproportionately destabilizing.

## E. The Saltroad Four

**Members:** Mara Vell — human from the Marrow Coast League, approximately 32, level-6 Fighter-style frontliner and captain. Brannik Coil — hill dwarf raised along the Rellin Coast, approximately 74, level-6 Cleric-style healer and defensive support. Tavi Reedfoot — lightfoot halfling from Gatehaven, a great western port, approximately 29, level-6 Rogue-style scout and negotiator. Ysra Dain — tiefling from the Sahran Principalities, approximately 27, level-6 Wizard-style utility caster and controller.
**Appearance:** Mara is sun-browned and practical, with a shaved sidecut and old caravan scars; Brannik is broad and cheerful-looking until fighting begins; Tavi dresses like a respectable trader rather than a thief; Ysra favors layered indigo travel clothes, brass spectacles, and ink-stained gloves.
**Equipment identity:** Balanced adventuring gear rather than bespoke elite kit: shield and longsword, travel-hammer and holy kit, thieves' tools and hand crossbow, spellbook and wand. They carry shared rope, poles, camp gear, spare rations, and too many labeled supply sacks.
**Why they entered:** They want money, fame, and the social jump that comes with becoming a name people recognize. They are tired of competent work earning competent pay while legends receive fortunes for appearing in the room. Tavi wants wealth, Ysra wants access to circles that currently ignore her, Mara wants to become a captain others seek out, and Brannik mostly wants to see whether his friends can actually pull it off. They are not embarrassed by ambition.
**How they know one another:** Six years of caravan defense, ruin-clearing, missing-person work, and monster contracts along the western trade roads.
**Intra-team relationship:** Genuine friendship with ordinary professional friction. Mara dislikes indecision, Tavi pushes for negotiation, Ysra is tempted by strange discoveries, and Brannik often keeps arguments from becoming personal.
**Leader:** Mara is the recognized captain, but they are accustomed to yielding authority to whoever owns the relevant problem.
**Behavior under fear:** They become more talkative and committee-driven. Their strength is cross-checking one another; their weakness is spending too long trying to find unanimous agreement.
**Killing contestants:** Prefer incapacitation, surrender, or disengagement when realistic. They will kill in self-defense and become less forgiving if another team proves repeatedly treacherous.
**Bargaining:** Comfortable and usually fair. Tavi enjoys turning temporary alignment of interests into short-term deals without pretending it creates friendship.
**If they lose a gem:** Initial frustration followed by a practical vote: pursue if the thief or victor is reachable, otherwise replace it through trade or another contest.
**If they gain several gems:** Confidence and public ambition rise quickly. They begin looking not only for qualification but for ways to make their success visible, profitable, and memorable. That can produce smart trading or tempt them into Spotlight decisions a more cautious team would decline.
**Attitude toward weaker teams:** Sympathetic without being self-sacrificing. They will share information, trade reasonably, and occasionally help at modest cost, but will not repeatedly endanger themselves for strangers.
**Route tendencies:** Generalists. They prefer routes with multiple problem types and avoid committing to highly specialized paths until they understand what lies ahead.
**Sponsor profile requirement:** Merchant consortium, adventuring guild, regional civic patron, or practical public sponsor that can provide supplies, replacement tools, and commercial contacts without trying to reshape the team's identity.
**Natural organiser fit:** Valerius becomes a natural favorite when they smell momentum and start performing for the audience; Nadira likes their commercial instincts and Tavi's bargaining. Metonius still respects their baseline professionalism, while Kessa can be irritated when fame starts overriding sensible risk. Arcos may warm to Mara if success makes her more assertive.
**Memorable voice/behavior:** They unconsciously discuss dangerous situations like paid contracts: "What's the objective, what's the risk, and who's covering the expense?" Tavi keeps a running chalk tally of favors owed on the inside of Mara's shield.
**Vulnerabilities:** No truly exceptional specialty; fear can turn healthy collaboration into hesitation, and splitting the party removes much of what makes them effective.

## F. The Farstriders

**Members:** Asha N'Kalo — human from the Keshari Basin, a tropical megafauna-rich jungle culture, approximately 31, level-8 Ranger-style tracker and expedition lead. Pellan Moss — firbolg from Elderbough's deep forest communities, approximately 67, level-7 Druid-style environmental controller and healer. Suri Quickstep — tabaxi from the Sunward Coast, a distant warm-water seafaring culture, approximately 28, level-8 Rogue-style scout and mobile ranged striker.
**Appearance:** Asha is compact, dark-skinned, and wears her hair tightly braided beneath a weather hood; Pellan is long-limbed, mossy-haired, and decorated with practical cords of seed, bone, and herb bundles; Suri is tawny-furred, narrow-faced, and almost constantly in motion.
**Equipment identity:** Light armor, bows, climbing harnesses, machetes, weatherproof packs, snares, water treatment, herbal kits, collapsible poles, and almost nothing they cannot carry at a run.
**Why they entered:** The three built a reputation guiding dangerous long-range expeditions where conventional parties failed. The Gauntlet is the ultimate proof of that profession, and a strong finish would fund years of independent exploration without relying on wealthy patrons who dictate destinations.
**How they know one another:** They first worked together during separate expeditions in the Keshari Basin and kept reforming as a team because their movement and survival skills complemented one another unusually well.
**Intra-team relationship:** Asha is the planner, Pellan is the brake on unnecessary risk, and Suri is the member most willing to exploit a fleeting opportunity. They trust one another strongly but disagree over how much uncertainty is acceptable.
**Leader:** Asha for route and pursuit decisions; no absolute command structure outside her specialty.
**Behavior under fear:** They spread out rather than bunch together, increase observation distance, and look for exits or terrain that breaks pursuit. To outsiders this can resemble abandonment even when they are executing a regroup plan.
**Killing contestants:** Prefer avoidance, ambush withdrawal, or disabling pressure. If another team persistently hunts them, they become willing to strike lethally from terrain advantage rather than accept repeated pursuit.
**Bargaining:** Favor route information, water, food, warnings, and temporary passage agreements over elaborate political deals.
**If they lose a gem:** Track first, fight second. They may follow a thief for hours or days looking for a low-risk recovery window rather than immediately confront them.
**If they gain several gems:** Spread risk across the members, reduce obvious camps, and seek efficient color trades. They dislike carrying more strategic weight than they can move quickly.
**Attitude toward weaker teams:** Often helpful with navigation or warnings, but reluctant to escort slower groups for long because doing so sacrifices their strongest advantage.
**Route tendencies:** Jungle, ravines, Warrens, ecological passages, animal routes, cliffs, and difficult terrain that punishes heavy groups. They are less comfortable in formal settlements, complex institutions, and highly engineered interiors.
**Sponsor profile requirement:** Expedition society, mapmaker, outfitter, naturalist institution, or frontier patron supplying light gear, environmental information, and specialist consumables without demanding static spectacle.
**Natural organiser fit:** Kessa is the strongest match; Nymor appreciates exploration, Metonius appreciates terrain use, and Sable may value unseen movement. Arcos and Valerius are easier to disappoint because the Farstriders often choose not to have the fight everyone expects.
**Memorable voice/behavior:** Asha rarely says "safe" and instead says "safer." Pellan touches walls, soil, water, and air before offering opinions. Suri answers questions while already moving toward the next vantage point.
**Vulnerabilities:** Confined engineered terrain weakens their mobility advantage; they lack a true durable frontline and can become socially outmaneuvered in places where route access depends on hierarchy, contract, or ritual.

## G. Borrowed Faces

**Members:** Ilyen Marr — human from Avarra's mercantile underworld, approximately 34, level-8 Rogue-style infiltrator and physical security specialist. Rhyss Varo — tiefling from Frostharbor, a hard northern freeport, approximately 30, level-8 Bard/Warlock-style deceiver, face, and control caster.
**Appearance:** Ilyen is deliberately forgettable at first glance: brown hair, ordinary build, neutral clothes, posture that changes with the room. Rhyss has swept-back dark horns, amber eyes, a quick smile, and the practiced elegance of someone who wants to be remembered for the wrong details.
**Equipment identity:** Reversible coats, disguise cosmetics, lock and trap tools, compact hand crossbows, wire, smoke and flash consumables, false pouches, concealed blades, and several objects whose primary purpose is to look more important than they are.
**Why they entered:** Their skills are criminal liabilities almost everywhere else and celebrated competencies inside the Gauntlet. A strong showing could convert years of illicit reputation into legitimate independence, wealth, and access to clients who hire rather than own them.
**How they know one another:** They met while independently trying to steal the same commercial ledger in Avarra. Each discovered the other, each nearly ruined the job, and the eventual shared escape became the beginning of a five-year partnership.
**Intra-team relationship:** Constant teasing hides extremely high operational trust. They routinely challenge one another's assumptions and maintain explicit rules for when one must abandon loot, plans, or even a rescue attempt rather than turn one capture into two.
**Leader:** None overall. Ilyen owns infiltration and physical security decisions; Rhyss owns conversation, deception, and magical misdirection.
**Behavior under fear:** Talk faster, lie more, create exits, and sacrifice possessions before mobility. When genuinely cornered their normal confidence can turn into excessive improvisation.
**Killing contestants:** Prefer not to. Corpses produce scrutiny and remove future sources of information. They become lethal when trapped, identified by a persistent hunter, or convinced that allowing an enemy to leave creates an unacceptable future risk.
**Bargaining:** Enthusiastic and dangerous. They honor the exact agreement they intended to make, which is not always the agreement the other party thought they heard.
**If they lose a gem:** Often easier to steal or trade for another than recover the original. A theft performed skillfully against them can, however, become personally difficult for them to ignore.
**If they gain several gems:** Reduce visible contacts, conceal their true count, use intermediaries where possible, and trade from information advantage rather than displaying wealth.
**Attitude toward weaker teams:** More likely to exploit them for information, access, or cover than attack them. They can be surprisingly gentle when cruelty offers no practical benefit.
**Route tendencies:** Hidden passages, Vent-City networks, social gates, crawlspaces, side routes, occupied settlements, and anywhere identity or access matters more than armor.
**Sponsor profile requirement:** Discreet intelligence, mercantile, criminal-adjacent, or political patron able to provide information, disguises, consumables, and plausible deniability without demanding unquestioning loyalty.
**Natural organiser fit:** Sable strongly; Nadira often appreciates leverage and Metonius respects clean execution. Arcos dislikes avoidance of direct dominance, while Veyra can object when manipulation treats vulnerable people as tools. Valerius may love a con if it becomes public theater.
**Memorable voice/behavior:** They sometimes complete one another's fabricated stories without prior planning. Ilyen watches hands; Rhyss watches who is watching Ilyen.
**Vulnerabilities:** Very low tolerance for being trapped in a fair sustained fight; disabling either member collapses much of their coverage, and institutions built around truth-magic or rigid ritual can neutralize their favorite tools.

## H. Copper Company

**Members:** Elra Tanner — human from a mixed farming settlement in the Greenvale Cantons, approximately 41, level-4 Fighter-style militia captain. Kipp Underbough — lightfoot halfling, same settlement, approximately 25, level-3 Rogue-style scout. Oren Feld — human, approximately 22, level-3 Ranger-style archer and forager. Mira Stonehand — hill dwarf whose family settled there two generations ago, approximately 58, level-3 Cleric-style healer. Joss Vale — human, approximately 20, level-3 Wizard-style apprentice. Grum Tal — orc, approximately 29, level-3 Barbarian/Fighter-style laborer and heavy frontliner.
**Appearance:** They look like competent local defenders rather than glamorous adventurers: patched mail, repaired leather, sun-faded cloaks, mismatched shields, and copper-colored armbands made by their home community.
**Equipment identity:** Practical militia and expedition gear, lots of rope, spare tools, bedrolls, cooking kit, bows, shields, simple healing supplies, and carefully maintained but mostly non-magical weapons. Their advantage is having six sets of hands, not elite equipment.
**Why they entered:** Their home survived a disastrous season of flooding, raids, and debt. The company believes even a partial Gauntlet success could transform the settlement's future; several members also see the Games as the first chance they have ever had to become more than local defenders.
**How they know one another:** Lifelong neighbors or near-neighbors who first fought together during local emergencies and then formed a volunteer adventuring company.
**Intra-team relationship:** Elra is the stabilizing adult presence. Kipp and Joss are close friends and sometimes encourage each other's worst ideas. Oren is the most eager to prove the company belongs among famous adventurers. Mira is trusted when tempers rise. Grum is quiet, dependable, and more perceptive about group morale than the others realize.
**Leader:** Elra. In immediate combat or disaster she expects obedience; outside crisis the group argues, votes, and complains freely.
**Behavior under fear:** They physically bunch toward Elra and Mira, reinforce formation, and become louder. If Elra is incapacitated, they risk splitting between "hold together" and "get out now" instincts.
**Killing contestants:** Reluctant. Their default assumption is that other contestants are people much like them. If one of their own is killed deliberately, that restraint can erode sharply and unevenly across the group.
**Bargaining:** Very willing, sometimes too willing. They understand practical exchange better than political games and can agree to obligations whose long-term cost they underestimate.
**If they lose a gem:** The company is likely to debate pursuit rather than immediately act. Oren and Kipp tend toward recovery; Elra and Mira are more willing to seek a replacement if pursuit risks lives.
**If they gain several gems:** Morale rises dramatically. That success can produce either disciplined caution or dangerous confidence depending on which members dominate the next decision.
**Attitude toward weaker teams:** Instinctively sympathetic and cooperative because they still think of themselves as one of the weaker teams. They may share food, information, or manpower more readily than is strategically wise.
**Route tendencies:** Broad stable routes, ordinary trails, defensible camps, spaces where six people can search or operate mechanisms simultaneously, and areas where mundane logistics matter.
**Sponsor profile requirement:** Civic, community, guild, or populist sponsor providing practical supplies and modest protective aid. A sponsor that treats individual members as expendable would rapidly lose the team's cooperation.
**Natural organiser fit:** Veyra, Kessa, and Nadira are natural positives; Metonius can reward Elra's discipline. Arcos may initially see them as unimpressive but can respect a brave stand. Valerius has obvious underdog-story potential without guaranteeing that the team survives long enough to become one.
**Memorable voice/behavior:** For non-urgent disputes Elra sometimes produces six worn copper discs and makes everyone vote. Joss still says "Captain" even when discussing breakfast.
**Vulnerabilities:** Area effects, fear, and high-save hazards can devastate several members at once; cohesion depends heavily on Elra and Mira, and their willingness to help others can overload already thin resources.

## I. Stonewake

**Members:** Borin Hearthmantle — mountain dwarf from the Ironroot Holds' engineering culture, approximately 103, level-6 Fighter-style shield anchor. Aruuk Sen — orc from a northern mountain settlement, approximately 38, level-6 Paladin/Fighter-style protector. Maelin Voss — human from Asterfall, a rebuilt northern river-city, approximately 36, level-6 Cleric-style healer and medical lead. Fennik Brasspin — rock gnome from the Brass Isles, famous for workshops and practical invention, approximately 54, level-5 Artificer/Expert-style engineer and utility specialist. Rill Aster — human from the Longwater Valley, approximately 30, level-5 Ranger-style scout, field medic, and route assessor.
**Appearance:** Borin is square-built and soot-grey with an iron-braided beard; Aruuk is tall, green-grey, and marked by old burn scars across one forearm; Maelin wears plain white-and-brown field vestments under armor; Fennik is perpetually dusted with chalk or grease; Rill has cropped blond hair, weathered skin, and a habit of tying colored route cords to equipment.
**Equipment identity:** Heavy shields, durable armor, medical packs, splints, rope systems, pulleys, pry bars, repair tools, portable warding materials, field stoves, and redundant supplies. Their gear looks like a rescue expedition designed to keep functioning after something goes wrong.
**Why they entered:** Stonewake is an established disaster-recovery and deep-expedition company. A Gauntlet victory would fund independent operations for years and prove that disciplined rescue-and-recovery doctrine can compete with faster, more violent adventuring styles.
**How they know one another:** Years of landslide, mine-collapse, ruin-recovery, avalanche, and monster-disaster contracts in different parts of the north brought them together gradually into a permanent professional team.
**Intra-team relationship:** Borin directs operations, Maelin has absolute authority over medical withdrawals, Rill can veto a route she believes cannot be safely returned through, Aruuk usually supports the most protective option, and Fennik habitually argues that one more piece of preparation will make a dangerous plan acceptable.
**Leader:** Borin overall, with formal specialist vetoes the others take seriously.
**Behavior under fear:** They slow down, count people, establish perimeter, triage injuries, and build fallback plans. This is usually excellent survival behavior but can become dangerously slow when the correct answer is immediate flight.
**Killing contestants:** Strongly prefer not to kill once an opponent is neutralized. They will fight lethally against a team that threatens patients, civilians, or repeatedly attacks after being given an exit.
**Bargaining:** Practical and dependable. Healing, repair, rescue, escort, and safe passage are all things they are comfortable trading for information, supplies, or gems.
**If they lose a gem:** A gem is never worth a team member's life. They first attempt negotiation or a controlled recovery; if the situation is poor, they redirect toward another copy.
**If they gain several gems:** They become more defensive and trade-focused rather than more predatory. Extra gems are leverage for obtaining missing colors, passage, or assistance.
**Attitude toward weaker teams:** Instinctively inclined to help, especially injured teams. They are capable of charging fair prices or setting limits, but repeated rescue obligations can trap them in responsibilities they did not intend to assume.
**Route tendencies:** Long attritional routes, hazardous engineered areas, places where preparation and recovery matter, and paths other teams abandon after accumulating injuries. Slow to choose routes dependent on stealth or explosive speed.
**Sponsor profile requirement:** Medical, engineering, mining, civic-rescue, or expedition patron able to provide consumables, tools, durable equipment, and logistical intelligence while respecting the team's professional safety doctrine.
**Natural organiser fit:** Veyra, Metonius, and Kessa strongly; Nadira appreciates dependable service exchanges. Arcos can find their caution excessive and Valerius may find methodical rescue work dull unless a crisis makes it visible.
**Memorable voice/behavior:** After every serious incident Borin counts people before equipment. Maelin corrects him if he reverses the order. Fennik says, "Give me ten minutes," with alarming frequency.
**Vulnerabilities:** Low burst damage and limited pursuit make it difficult to end fast-moving threats; compassion and professional rescue ethics can be manipulated, and their instinct to stabilize a situation can become dangerous when the terrain itself must be abandoned immediately.

## J. The Falling Road

**Members:** Senn Taal — human from a mountain-monastery tradition in the Skybreak Range, approximately 36, level-9 Monk-style mobile striker and climber. Iriane Fen — wood elf from the Stormglass Isles, approximately 112, level-8 Druid-style shapeshifter, environmental caster, and traversal specialist.
**Appearance:** Senn is lean, shaved-headed, weather-darkened, and usually barefoot when terrain permits, with old rope-burn scars across both palms. Iriane has dark green-black hair, angular features, a weather cloak patched with different local fabrics, and carries very little that cannot survive getting wet.
**Equipment identity:** Extremely light loads: climbing cord, hooked staff, compact spear, water skins, weatherproof wraps, herbal kit, small ritual components, and a flat river-stone druidic focus. They regard heavy equipment as terrain that follows you around.
**Why they entered:** They have spent seven years taking contracts to reach places ordinary rescue teams, messengers, or explorers cannot. The Gauntlet is the most famous traversal challenge in the world, and a strong result would establish their tiny two-person service as something no government or guild could dismiss as a curiosity.
**How they know one another:** They met during an earthquake rescue in which each independently reached a cliff settlement by a route everyone else considered impossible. Their methods were different enough to be complementary rather than competitive.
**Intra-team relationship:** Senn's instinct is "forward until the route proves impossible"; Iriane's is "know how to return before committing." They argue frequently about acceptable irreversibility but trust each other's movement judgment almost completely.
**Leader:** No permanent leader. Senn takes point on physical crossings and immediate movement; Iriane leads environmental assessment, magical traversal, and route commitment.
**Behavior under fear:** They move. Height, distance, broken sightlines, and changing terrain are their preferred defenses. Immobilization of either member is the condition most likely to produce poor decisions from the other.
**Killing contestants:** Usually avoid it because they can escape encounters many teams cannot. If trapped, restrained, or repeatedly pursued, they switch rapidly from avoidance to concentrated violence intended to reopen movement.
**Bargaining:** Prefer short-term trades: route information, passage, warning, a temporary guide, a color exchange. They dislike large coalitions and promises that constrain where they can go next.
**If they lose a gem:** They will not sacrifice their mobility advantage merely to chase the current holder. They are more likely to shadow, wait for a favorable recovery opportunity, or seek another copy.
**If they gain several gems:** Trade duplicates quickly when useful and remain mobile. They resist strategies requiring them to defend a fixed cache or rendezvous point for long periods.
**Attitude toward weaker teams:** Will often show someone a route, lower a rope, or intervene in an immediate rescue, but are reluctant to absorb slower teams into their travel plan. They distinguish helping someone survive a moment from becoming responsible for their journey.
**Route tendencies:** Cliffs, shafts, broken bridges, canopy, vertical infrastructure, narrow ledges, wild-shape bypasses, and physically intimidating shortcuts. They avoid routes whose challenge is primarily holding ground or carrying large amounts of material.
**Sponsor profile requirement:** Mobility, expedition, courier, druidic, or specialist-equipment patron supplying lightweight consumables, maps, climbing aids, and flexible magical support. Bulky prestige equipment is almost useless to them.
**Natural organiser fit:** Kessa strongly; Nymor likes unconventional discovery and Metonius respects repositioning. Valerius can adore spectacular movement under Spotlight. Arcos may dislike their refusal of stand-up contests, while Nadira may find them frustratingly unwilling to bind themselves to long agreements.
**Memorable voice/behavior:** Iriane's first question about any shortcut is, "How do we come back?" Senn tests unfamiliar stone with the edge of his bare foot and tends to smile only after a route becomes genuinely dangerous.
**Vulnerabilities:** Restraint, anti-magic, and environments that remove meaningful movement options; low carrying capacity and limited ability to evacuate an incapacitated ally make rescue situations disproportionately dangerous.

## Emergent-play requirement

These profiles define motives, habits, fault lines, and conditional responses. They do not prescribe future events. A team's relationship with the players must arise from actual encounters, resource state, injuries, gem pressure, organiser influence, and choices made during play.

These profiles are now reviewed and canonical. Sponsor design may begin, with each sponsor relationship built around specific contestants to support, pressure, exploit, misunderstand, or depend upon rather than functioning as a generic equipment channel.

## Roster variance check

The ten primary teams are not intended to share one adventuring moral code. Current deliberate contrasts include:
- The Last Standard: prestige, dominance, and public proof; coercive toward weaker teams without being sadistic.
- The Black Banner: national/frontier prestige and the strongest acceptance of preventative contestant killing.
- The Saltroad Four: openly motivated by wealth, fame, access, and upward mobility; increasingly Spotlight-sensitive when successful.
- Borrowed Faces: transactional information advantage and manipulation, with little moral attachment to fair dealing beyond the exact agreement they believe they made.
- Third Bell, Copper Company, The Glass Star, The Farstriders, Stonewake, and The Falling Road retain their stronger existing cores rather than being darkened merely for symmetry.

All Eight organisers now have plausible natural favorites somewhere among A-J. This is not a prediction of earned Favor; actual Favor remains event-driven.
