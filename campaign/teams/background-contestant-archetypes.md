# Background Contestant Archetypes

Status: Working mechanical draft. These are the 14 lower-fidelity rival entries that complete the 24-rival field around the player team.

## Design intent

These entries are not disposable extras and they are not fourteen weaker copies of the primary roster. Each represents a mechanical shape that can plausibly succeed or fail differently inside the Gauntlet.

At this stage, do not assign names, ancestry/species, homeland, culture, appearance, detailed motives, sponsor identity, personality, subclass, exact spell list, or final equipment.

The useful information now is team size, approximate level-equivalent, combat shape, recovery, exploration toolkit, route suitability, and mechanical failure points.

Threat labels are relative to four player characters at the stated level and assume reasonably fresh resources on neutral terrain. Prepared terrain, surprise, spell depletion, injuries, or environmental advantage can move a team one or more bands.

The entries are lettered K-X to continue the primary roster A-J.

## K. Defensive shield group

**Members:** 4.
**Mechanical model:** Three martial defenders around level 5 plus one level 4-5 support/healer equivalent.
**Combat shape:** High AC, protection reactions, formation fighting, moderate damage, limited ranged pressure.
**Threat trend:** Level 5 Peer; level 6 Peer/Inferior; levels 7-8 Inferior unless defending a choke point.
**Arena strengths:** Holding doors and bridges, protecting a wounded member, resisting ordinary melee attrition, organized retreat.
**Arena weaknesses:** Dexterity-heavy hazards, stealth, vertical movement, magical bypasses, enemies who refuse to fight the formation.
**Route preference:** Broad engineered passages, defensible corridors, bridges, dwarven roads, stable surface trails.
## L. Arcane strike cell

**Members:** 3.
**Mechanical model:** Three full-caster or caster-heavy contestants around level 6.
**Combat shape:** High first-round control and area damage, multiple concentration effects, low total HP, poor sustained frontline presence.
**Threat trend:** Level 5 Superior when fresh; level 6 Peer/Superior; level 7 Peer; level 8 Inferior/Peer depending remaining spell resources.
**Arena strengths:** Magical barriers, flight or levitation, detection, ranged control, solving some environmental problems without touching them.
**Arena weaknesses:** Rest dependence, concentration pressure, physical capture, anti-magic, repeated medium encounters, carrying incapacitated allies.
**Route preference:** Routes where magic can bypass obstacles and where safe resting opportunities are available.

## M. Prepared monster-hunter party

**Members:** 4.
**Mechanical model:** Four level 5-6 martial/expert/half-caster equivalents built around scouting, ranged attacks, traps, and creature knowledge.
**Combat shape:** Average in an unexpected duel, much stronger with time to observe and prepare a target.
**Threat trend:** Level 5 Peer; level 6 Peer; levels 7-8 Inferior in general combat, but can remain Peer against a creature or team they have specifically prepared for.
**Arena strengths:** Tracking, ambush preparation, identifying monster behavior, harvesting useful materials, avoiding territorial predators.
**Arena weaknesses:** Improvisational magic, social gates, puzzles outside their specialty, enemies that change tactics quickly.
**Route preference:** Creature territories, Great Warrens, jungle hunting paths, large ecological spaces.
## N. Recovery-heavy expedition

**Members:** 4.
**Mechanical model:** Four level 4-5 support/divine/primal equivalents with unusually strong healing, stabilization, condition removal, and defensive magic.
**Combat shape:** Low burst damage but difficult to wear down through ordinary attrition.
**Threat trend:** Level 5 Inferior/Peer; level 6 Inferior; levels 7-8 Fragile in a direct fight, while remaining unusually resilient across multiple rooms.
**Arena strengths:** Long adventuring days, disease/poison recovery, rescuing incapacitated members, surviving environmental chip damage.
**Arena weaknesses:** Killing hard targets quickly, pursuit, stealth, opponents who can isolate or incapacitate the healers simultaneously.
**Route preference:** Hazardous long routes that punish resource depletion more than single-encounter power.

## O. Sapper and engineer trio

**Members:** 3.
**Mechanical model:** Three level 5-6 expert/martial/utility-caster equivalents focused on tools, demolition, traps, structural assessment, and mechanical systems.
**Combat shape:** Inferior in an unprepared open fight; Peer or better when they have time to rig terrain, establish cover, or control a doorway.
**Threat trend:** Level 5 Inferior/Peer; level 6 Inferior; levels 7-8 Fragile in neutral combat.
**Arena strengths:** Breaching blocked routes, disabling mechanisms, building anchors and bridges, manipulating dwarven infrastructure, setting traps.
**Arena weaknesses:** Magical puzzles, open wilderness, sudden attacks, low redundancy if the principal engineer is lost.
**Route preference:** Broken Dwarven Realm, collapsed infrastructure, ruined bridges, mines, mechanical gates, damaged lifts.
## P. Beast-handler pack

**Members:** 3 contestants plus roughly 3 trained combat or utility companions.
**Mechanical model:** Contestants around level 4-5 with companion-focused features; companions should matter tactically but remain substantially easier to kill than the handlers.
**Combat shape:** Six bodies create strong screening, flanking, tracking, and pursuit pressure despite modest individual power.
**Threat trend:** Level 5 Peer; level 6 Inferior/Peer; levels 7-8 Inferior, with a sharp drop if companions are lost.
**Arena strengths:** Scent/tracking, perimeter security, searching large spaces, pursuit, carrying light loads, detecting hidden creatures.
**Arena weaknesses:** Area damage, fear effects, narrow passages, ladders/shafts, environments companions cannot physically traverse, food burden.
**Route preference:** Jungle, broad Warrens, Verdant Expanse, surface trails, other spaces where companions can maneuver.

## Q. Seven-member low-level company

**Members:** 7.
**Mechanical model:** One level 4 leader, two level 3 specialists, and four level 2-3 martial or expert equivalents.
**Combat shape:** Individually weak but dangerous if they can spread out, concentrate ranged attacks, grapple isolated targets, or occupy several positions at once.
**Threat trend:** Level 5 Inferior; level 6 Fragile/Inferior; levels 7-8 Fragile.
**Arena strengths:** Watch rotations, carrying casualties, operating simultaneous mechanisms, searching, camp labor, redundancy after minor losses.
**Arena weaknesses:** Area effects, high save DCs, fear, difficult logistics, noisy movement, supply demand, narrow routes.
**Route preference:** Wide, understandable routes where numbers remain useful and catastrophic single-save hazards can be avoided.
## R. Apprentice magical expedition

**Members:** 5.
**Mechanical model:** Five level 3-4 caster or half-caster equivalents with broad low-tier utility but little high-level magic.
**Combat shape:** Many cantrips, reactions, utility effects, and low-level area spells; very low individual durability.
**Threat trend:** Level 5 Inferior; levels 6-8 Fragile, although poor positioning by the players can still make five spellcasting turns dangerous.
**Arena strengths:** Ritual breadth, light, detection, minor elemental manipulation, communication, magical experimentation, covering several utility niches.
**Arena weaknesses:** Low HP, weak concentration, poor physical saves, limited high-level answers, catastrophic losses from powerful area effects.
**Route preference:** Puzzle-heavy or magical routes where breadth of small solutions matters more than raw spell tier.

## S. Veteran mercenary company

**Members:** 5.
**Mechanical model:** Five level 4-5 martial/expert equivalents with disciplined formations, ranged weapons, field medicine, and mundane scouting but no full caster.
**Combat shape:** Reliable physical damage, coordinated focus fire, strong watches and camp discipline, limited magical answers.
**Threat trend:** Level 5 Peer; level 6 Inferior/Peer; levels 7-8 Inferior.
**Arena strengths:** Endurance, discipline, marching, mundane logistics, defending camps, fighting ordinary creatures without spending scarce magic.
**Arena weaknesses:** Curses, magical control, planar geometry, strong elemental hazards, inaccessible vertical terrain.
**Route preference:** Stable roads, surface tracks, dwarven infrastructure, defensible camps, routes that reward discipline over magical improvisation.
## T. Solo sanctified champion

**Members:** 1.
**Mechanical model:** Level 9 paladin/cleric-style martial-support equivalent.
**Combat shape:** High defenses, strong saving throws, self-healing, limited burst damage, excellent resistance to fear/undead/corruption effects, weak action economy.
**Threat trend:** Level 5 Superior; level 6 Superior/Peer; level 7 Peer; level 8 Inferior/Peer depending terrain.
**Arena strengths:** Surviving curses and undead regions, self-sufficiency, emergency healing, strong morale effects, physically forcing a route.
**Arena weaknesses:** Stealth, scouting, simultaneous mechanisms, carrying another incapacitated contestant, ranged pursuit, complex arcane puzzles.
**Route preference:** Dangerous sacred, haunted, or corrupted routes that discourage more fragile contestants.

## U. Lone returning survivor

**Members:** 1.
**Mechanical model:** Level 8 all-round adventurer equivalent with moderate combat power and unusually broad survival/navigation competence.
**Combat shape:** Less explosive than the other solo entries; wins through preparation, retreat timing, resource economy, and knowing when not to fight.
**Threat trend:** Level 5 Peer/Superior in favorable terrain; level 6 Peer; level 7 Inferior/Peer; level 8 Inferior in direct combat.
**Arena strengths:** Prior-Games judgment, route recognition, camp discipline, reading arena behavior, identifying rooms that are likely to be disproportionately lethal.
**Arena weaknesses:** One body, previous knowledge can be obsolete, limited answers to newly opened routes, no redundancy after a failed save or serious injury.
**Route preference:** Previously reliable paths, conservative bypasses, and routes with known fallback options.
## V. Long-range kill pair

**Members:** 2.
**Mechanical model:** Two level 8-9 ranged/control specialists; one focuses on precision damage and the other on spotting, slowing, concealment, or battlefield control.
**Combat shape:** Extremely dangerous across open sightlines, much weaker when rushed, surrounded, blinded, or forced into confined melee.
**Threat trend:** Level 5 Superior on favorable terrain; level 6 Superior/Peer; level 7 Peer; level 8 Inferior/Peer.
**Arena strengths:** Observation, overwatch, denying crossings, hunting exposed targets, controlling long bridges/ridges/caverns without occupying them physically.
**Arena weaknesses:** Close quarters, heavy obscurement, teleporting/flanking opponents, simultaneous pressure from multiple directions, small team size.
**Route preference:** Ridges, long halls, cavern ledges, open jungle breaks, bridges, any route offering sightline control.

## W. Summoner and bodyguard pair

**Members:** 2.
**Mechanical model:** One level 9 summoning/control caster equivalent and one level 8 durable defender equivalent.
**Combat shape:** When fresh, summoned creatures or controlled entities temporarily solve the pair's action-economy weakness; once concentration or major spell resources are gone, their power drops sharply.
**Threat trend:** Level 5 Overmatch when fully resourced; level 6 Superior; level 7 Peer/Superior; level 8 Peer, with depletion capable of dropping them another band.
**Arena strengths:** Disposable scouting bodies, battlefield control, temporary extra hands, blocking corridors, testing dangerous spaces without exposing both contestants.
**Arena weaknesses:** Concentration disruption, resource attrition, anti-magic, cramped routes, bodyguard separation, effects that remove the caster before summons matter.
**Route preference:** Dangerous unknown spaces where temporary creatures can probe ahead and direct fighting can be delegated.
## X. Ward and survey trio

**Members:** 3.
**Mechanical model:** Three level 6 support/expert/caster equivalents built around warding, trap detection, ritual utility, environmental resistance, and emergency recovery.
**Combat shape:** Low burst damage and only moderate frontline strength, but strong defensive reactions and unusually good answers to hazards.
**Threat trend:** Level 5 Peer; level 6 Inferior/Peer; levels 7-8 Inferior.
**Arena strengths:** Detecting magical danger, resisting elemental exposure, curses and traps, route surveying, cautious experimentation, preserving resources through hazardous nodes.
**Arena weaknesses:** Finishing powerful enemies, pursuit, open-field damage races, being surrounded, problems that demand raw strength or many simultaneous bodies.
**Route preference:** Veiled, buried, ash-labyrinth, and mixed historical routes where investigation and protection matter more than speed.
## Background fidelity rule

K-X receive enough mechanical definition to resolve abstract movement and to run a credible encounter if they appear unexpectedly. They do not yet require individual personalities, complete stat blocks, exact subclasses, personal histories, or bespoke relationship arcs.

Promote a background entry when repeated player contact, unusual survival, important gem ownership, a major alliance or conflict, or a striking arena event makes continued abstraction artificial.

Primary entries receive no survival protection. A background entry that remains relevant through play can naturally take greater campaign importance.

## Full-field size balance

Across all 24 rival entries, the intended shape is approximately:
- 4 solo entries: A, B, T, U.
- 4 clear two-person entries: C, G, V, W.
- 5-6 three-person entries: D, F, O, P, X, plus J if finalized at three.
- 4 four-person entries: E, K, M, N.
- 3 five-person entries: I, R, S.
- 2 large companies: H at six members and Q at seven.

J remains deliberately flexible at two or three members until its final movement toolkit is selected.

This yields roughly 75-80 rival contestants before trained companions or temporary summoned creatures. The Games register competitive teams rather than enforcing a standardized party size.

At level 5, the baseline field is intentionally broad: 4 Overmatch entries, 5 Superior entries, 10 Peer entries, and 5 Inferior entries. Situational Fragile states exist, but no contestant entry is designed as harmless at insertion.

By level 8, player advancement changes the relationship without automatically making the arena safe: approximately 2 Superior entries, 7 Peer entries, 10 Inferior entries, and 5 Fragile entries remain before accounting for injuries, resources, alliances, terrain, or rival advancement.

This shift is desirable. Early in the Games, many contestants are unknown quantities and several are plainly beyond the party in direct combat. By the qualification race, the player team has grown into one of the stronger competitive units, while still having reasons to respect elite survivors and favorable matchups.

These labels do not mean rivals remain mechanically frozen. Equipment gains, injuries, lost members, sponsor aid, major discoveries, and limited advancement can alter an entry. Any such change should be explicit rather than silently level-scaling every rival to the players.
