# Contestant Field Framework — Mechanical Revision

Status: Mechanical framework approved for primary-team character creation. Exact insertion placement and gem assignment remain separate workstreams.

## Core field principle

The Games contain 25 registered teams total, including the player team. A "team" is a competitive entry, not a promise of four characters at the same level.

The contestant field should be deliberately uneven. Plausible entries include:
- One experienced adventurer operating alone.
- A pair of very high-level veterans.
- A conventional three-to-five-person adventuring party.
- A larger company of individually weaker contestants.
- A specialist group whose combat power is modest but whose traversal, magic, scouting, or survival capability is exceptional.

The player party is expected to contain four characters progressing roughly from levels 5 to 8. Rival contestants do not scale to match them.

Most teams are strangers. Contestants travel from many nations, cultures, adventuring traditions, military orders, guilds, temples, criminal circles, and isolated regions to enter the Games. Shared histories between rival teams should be exceptional rather than the default.

One or two rival entries may be returning contestants or contain previous participants. That gives the field a small amount of prior Games experience without making the competition feel like a closed social circle.

Primary-team status means mechanical and runtime fidelity, not plot importance or survival protection. A primary rival may die in its first dangerous room; a background team may become important through play.## Mechanical authoring rule

Class levels are useful during design because they communicate expected spell access, martial progression, hit-point scale, saving throws, mobility, and signature abilities. They are not treated as direct CR conversions.

For actual hostile or allied encounters, recurring contestants should normally receive streamlined NPC stat blocks inspired by a class rather than full player-character sheets. Full PC sheets should be reserved for exceptional rivals where the added complexity is worthwhile.

A rival stat block should preserve the recognizable class fantasy and the handful of mechanics that define how that contestant fights. A fighter-derived rival should feel like an experienced fighter; a rogue-derived rival should win through position and burst; a wizard-derived rival should alter the encounter through spell choice rather than carrying an entire player spellbook.

Contestants are not designed as fair encounters. The arena can place the player team against opponents they should avoid, bargain with, ambush, escape from, or exploit indirectly. Likewise, some contestants should be clearly weaker than the player party.

Mechanical threat must also remain multidimensional. A high-level martial contestant may dominate an ordinary fight yet be poor at curses, magical navigation, social access, environmental survival, or ancient puzzles. A weaker team may survive unusually well because it has excellent scouting, healing, utility magic, climbing, stealth, or route knowledge.

The volcano itself remains dangerous enough to kill strong contestants. Rival power should never imply immunity to authored room hazards, Extreme nodes, attrition, isolation, bad decisions, or hostile local societies.## Recommended recurring fidelity

Keep 10 mechanically authored rival entries at primary fidelity, plus the player team, leaving 14 background entries.

Ten still works, but for a different reason than the previous draft: it is enough to expose the table to several genuinely different power bands and party structures without requiring 24 complete stat packages before play begins.

The ten primary entries should approximately cover:
- 2 opponents substantially above the player party's normal combat weight.
- 2 opponents somewhat above the player party.
- 3 broadly peer-band teams whose relative danger changes as the party levels.
- 2 weaker or numerically awkward teams that the players can plausibly dominate.
- 1 mechanically unusual team whose survival comes from mobility, stealth, utility, or arena knowledge rather than straightforward DPR.

This distribution is intentionally not symmetrical. The wider 14-team background field should add more weak teams, ordinary adventuring parties, dangerous specialists, and occasional elite entries.

### Threat labels for contestant design

Use broad relative labels instead of assuming encounter balance:
- **Overmatch:** direct combat is likely catastrophic without a major advantage.
- **Superior:** winnable under favorable circumstances, but clearly more dangerous than a normal peer fight.
- **Peer:** broadly comparable combat weight at the relevant campaign stage.
- **Inferior:** weaker than the player party in straightforward combat but still capable of escape, negotiation, traps, or opportunism.
- **Fragile:** badly outmatched in direct combat and dependent on avoidance, numbers, positioning, or alliances.

These labels should be reevaluated as the player party moves from level 5 to level 8.## First-pass primary mechanical roster

These are mechanical contestant slots, not final team brands. Final individuals should receive names, ancestry, homeland, culture, appearance, motives, and personality only after the mechanical field is accepted.

### A. Lone veteran champion

**Members:** 1.
**Mechanical model:** Fighter 12 or Barbarian 12 equivalent.
**Encounter profile:** Approximately AC 18-20, 120-150 HP, strong physical saves, multiple attacks, one major burst/recovery feature, limited ranged or magical utility.
**Relative threat:** Overmatch for four level-5 characters if encountered fresh and on favorable terrain; increasingly manageable through action economy by level 8.
**Arena strengths:** Direct combat, forced marches, climbing, carrying capacity, breaking physical obstacles, surviving ordinary attrition.
**Arena weaknesses:** One body, limited skill coverage, control magic, complex magical hazards, simultaneous objectives, no one to stabilize or rescue them if incapacitated.
**Route behavior:** Prefers routes where strength and durability can substitute for finesse. May abandon puzzle-heavy or socially gated routes even when physically short.
**Starting need:** No special story placement; a harsh physical start demonstrates why a lone contestant can plausibly survive.

### B. Lone high-tier spellcaster

**Members:** 1.
**Mechanical model:** Wizard 11, Sorcerer 11, or Warlock 11 equivalent.
**Encounter profile:** Roughly AC 15-18, 80-110 HP, spell save DC around the high teens, access to magic substantially beyond the level-5 party's normal counterplay, but limited daily resources and poor action economy if pinned down.
**Relative threat:** Potential Overmatch because one high-level control or damage spell can decide an encounter; far less secure if surprised, exhausted, silenced, restrained, or forced through multiple encounters without recovery.
**Arena strengths:** Magical bypasses, scouting, flight or unusual movement, countering hazards, ranged control, information gathering.
**Arena weaknesses:** Resource attrition, concentration pressure, physical capture, carrying/rescue problems, anti-magic, hazards that require multiple people in different places.
**Route behavior:** Can take routes other contestants consider inaccessible, but may deliberately seek safe rests and avoid attritional regions.### C. Elite veteran pair

**Members:** 2.
**Mechanical model:** Two characters around levels 10-11; for example Paladin + Ranger, Fighter + Cleric, or Rogue + Sorcerer.
**Encounter profile:** Each should have a clear battlefield role. Together they possess enough durability, burst, and save coverage that fighting both is considerably worse than facing either alone.
**Relative threat:** Usually Overmatch at level 5 and Superior at level 8 unless the party creates separation, surprise, environmental leverage, or a resource advantage.
**Arena strengths:** Excellent mutual support, enough skill diversity to cover many ordinary rooms, easier stealth/logistics than a large party, high individual competence.
**Arena weaknesses:** Losing either member destroys much of the team's action economy and coverage; simultaneous objectives and prolonged attrition are serious problems.
**Route behavior:** Can take dangerous direct routes that ordinary parties avoid, but are less willing to risk either member on uncertain puzzles or sacrificial mechanics.
**Starting need:** A start with a clearly dangerous exit is appropriate; they do not need proximity to the players.

### D. Returning Games trio

**Members:** 3.
**Mechanical model:** Approximately levels 8/8/7, class mix such as Fighter, Rogue, and Wizard or Cleric.
**Encounter profile:** Strong enough to threaten a level-5 party, but their defining advantage is previous Gauntlet experience rather than raw level alone.
**Relative threat:** Superior early; closer to Peer by level 8 if they have not improved materially.
**Arena strengths:** Known route families, understanding of contestant behavior, practiced retreat procedures, better judgment about which rooms are not worth entering.
**Arena weaknesses:** Old route knowledge may be wrong, they may over-trust patterns from a previous Games, and they can still be surprised by living cultures or newly opened paths.
**Route behavior:** Favors routes that were reliable in the prior Games and bypasses places they remember as disproportionately lethal.
**Starting need:** None mechanically. Their previous knowledge should matter after breakout rather than handing them an automatic good insertion point.

Only one primary team needs this returning-participant role by default. A second returning entry can exist in the background field if desired.### E. Conventional peer adventuring party

**Members:** 4.
**Mechanical model:** Four characters around level 6 at campaign start; a recognizable balanced spread such as Fighter, Cleric, Rogue, and Wizard/Sorcerer.
**Encounter profile:** The closest rival to a normal player party. They have healing, scouting, frontline presence, utility, and enough actions to use terrain effectively.
**Relative threat:** Peer to a level-5 party but may begin slightly favored through level advantage; becomes Inferior if they remain static while the players advance toward level 8.
**Arena strengths:** Broad skill coverage, redundancy, conventional dungeon-solving ability, reliable combat formation.
**Arena weaknesses:** No exceptional specialty, substantial food/rest needs, ordinary mobility, vulnerable to being pulled apart.
**Route behavior:** Can use almost any normal route and therefore functions as an excellent baseline competitor in the simulation.
**Starting need:** Any ordinary Jungle Ring territory.

### F. Wilderness and pursuit trio

**Members:** 3.
**Mechanical model:** Ranger 8, Rogue 7-8, Druid 7 or similarly mobile nature-heavy combination.
**Encounter profile:** Moderate raw durability but excellent initiative, stealth, ranged pressure, scouting, escape, and environmental control.
**Relative threat:** Superior in jungle, Warrens, vertical terrain, or prepared ambushes; closer to Peer in confined stand-up fights.
**Arena strengths:** Tracking, concealment, foraging, difficult terrain, finding creature routes, avoiding unnecessary encounters.
**Arena weaknesses:** Lower staying power in sustained melee, fewer bodies for simultaneous tasks, weaker social/institutional access.
**Route behavior:** Favors natural/ecological routes, animal trails, jungle penetrations, Great Warrens, and bypasses around engineered choke points.
**Starting need:** Dense jungle, ravine, river, or creature-route access.

### G. Infiltration pair

**Members:** 2.
**Mechanical model:** Rogue 8 plus Warlock/Bard/Sorcerer 8, or two similarly stealth-oriented specialists.
**Encounter profile:** Dangerous first round, strong disengagement, deception, concealment, and targeted control; poor sustained frontline presence.
**Relative threat:** Peer or Superior if they choose the engagement; Inferior if trapped in a fair stand-up fight against four healthy PCs.
**Arena strengths:** Stealing gems, route notes, and supplies; scouting occupied rooms; bypassing locks and social barriers; escaping.
**Arena weaknesses:** Low total hit-point pool, little redundancy, weak carrying/rescue capacity, catastrophic consequences if one member is disabled.
**Route behavior:** Favors hidden, social, narrow, Vent-City, and side-route networks rather than obvious main corridors.
**Starting need:** Broken terrain with several concealment or traversal options.### H. Large low-level company

**Members:** 6.
**Mechanical model:** Six contestants around levels 3-4, or equivalent Veteran/Scout/Acolyte-style NPC stat blocks with one slightly stronger leader.
**Encounter profile:** Individually weak, but six turns per round make them dangerous if they concentrate fire, hold a choke point, or surround isolated targets.
**Relative threat:** Usually Inferior against four healthy level-5+ PCs in an open fight, but action economy prevents them from being harmless.
**Arena strengths:** Watch rotations, carrying wounded members, simultaneous tasks, searching large areas, redundancy, mundane logistics.
**Arena weaknesses:** Area damage, fear/control effects, poor high-level saves, expensive supplies, noisy travel, narrow routes, stronger monsters can remove members quickly.
**Route behavior:** Favors broad, understandable, defensible routes and avoids tight vents, risky climbs, and rooms where one failed save can destroy half the team.
**Starting need:** Relatively navigable surface territory rather than an insertion requiring immediate specialist movement.

### I. Durable expedition party

**Members:** 5.
**Mechanical model:** Five contestants around levels 5-6 with two durable frontliners and strong healing/support, but only moderate burst damage.
**Encounter profile:** Hard to finish quickly. Their strength is resource conversion and recovery rather than winning initiative and deleting targets.
**Relative threat:** Peer early; potentially Inferior offensively by level 8 while remaining expensive and dangerous to fight.
**Arena strengths:** Attrition resistance, stabilizing injuries, escorting wounded members, defensive camps, long exploration days.
**Arena weaknesses:** Slow pursuit, limited stealth, less magical bypass, weaker ability to end a dangerous fight immediately.
**Route behavior:** Can survive long difficult routes and repeated medium threats that exhaust more explosive teams.
**Starting need:** A resource-poor or hazardous insertion region showcases their mechanical advantage without granting a route advantage.

### J. Extreme-mobility specialists

**Members:** 2-3.
**Mechanical model:** Examples include Monk 9 + Druid 8, Ranger 8 + Monk 8, or another combination with exceptional speed, climbing, flight, shapechanging, or vertical traversal.
**Encounter profile:** Moderate direct combat power but difficult to pin down. Their strongest ability is treating geography as less restrictive than other contestants do.
**Relative threat:** Usually Peer in combat, but strategically Superior on cliff, shaft, ridge, and broken-infrastructure maps.
**Arena strengths:** Escape, vertical access, scouting, reaching mechanisms, crossing gaps, taking routes that are impractical for armored groups.
**Arena weaknesses:** Small team size, limited carrying capacity, vulnerable when terrain no longer permits movement, modest sustained healing unless one member provides it.
**Route behavior:** Strong preference for vertical connectors and physically difficult shortcuts rather than safe main routes.
**Starting need:** High ridge, ravine, cliff, broken bridge, or other terrain where mobility produces options without guaranteeing progress.## Background field: 14 mechanically lightweight entries

The secondary field is now defined in campaign/teams/background-contestant-archetypes.md. Entries K-X complete the mechanically authored 24-rival field around the player team.

Background status is a fidelity distinction, not a power ranking. The secondary entries include dangerous specialists and elite contestants as well as weaker companies. They receive enough mechanical identity to resolve route survival, attrition, and unexpected encounters without requiring full individual NPC authoring.

The background document also records the baseline threat distribution at player levels 5 and 8. Threat is never a substitute for actual terrain, resources, preparation, or party composition.

## Worldwide contestant identity

The field should look internationally assembled rather than locally networked.

During individual-character creation, distribute the ten primary entries across distinct homelands, cultures, institutions, and adventuring traditions. Avoid clustering most contestants around Waterdeep simply because it is a known staging location.

Cross-team familiarity should be rare:
- Most primary teams should have no meaningful history with one another before insertion.
- One or two may recognize another contestant by reputation without knowing them personally.
- Only the returning Games entries are expected to have direct prior knowledge of other contestants from an earlier Gauntlet.
- Any pre-existing rivalry, debt, family link, or professional history should be unusual enough to matter when discovered.

This means relationships normally begin during the current Games through observed competence, combat, rescue, theft, trade, betrayal, gem possession, or shared survival.

## Recurring-team runtime state

Track primary contestants mechanically as well as narratively:
- Each member's class/archetype, effective level/CR shorthand, combat role, AC band, HP condition, and key save strengths/weaknesses.
- Signature limited-use resources that materially affect the next encounter.
- Spellcasting tier and whether major spell resources are depleted.
- Functional roles currently available: healer, scout, controller, frontline, ranged pressure, utility caster, face, navigator, specialist movement.
- Injuries, exhaustion, diseases, curses, lost equipment, and member separation.
- Current overall relative threat band versus the player party.
- Current location, intended route, and mechanical reason that route suits them.
- True gem inventory and player-known/rumored gem information.
- Route knowledge and room-specific information.
- Current willingness to fight, retreat, negotiate, cooperate, or exploit another team.

A casualty should force reassessment. A five-person team that loses its only healer or scout is not mechanically the same team with one fewer token.## Arena difficulty implications

Contestant capability and room difficulty should interact rather than cancel each other out.

Examples:
- A level-12 martial solo may kill ordinary monsters quickly but still fail a magical sequence requiring several simultaneous positions.
- An elite pair may dominate a normal skirmish but be unwilling to risk one member crossing an unknown one-way portal.
- A six-person low-level company can operate mechanisms, maintain watches, and carry casualties, but a single strong area effect or high-save hazard may cripple it.
- A level-11 caster can bypass cliffs or sealed doors while fresh, then become dramatically less capable after spending high-tier magic to survive earlier rooms.
- A wilderness trio can cross hostile jungle cheaply yet become badly disadvantaged in a social settlement, formal duel, dwarven mechanism, or anti-magic location.
- A balanced level-6 party may be less spectacular than elite contestants but have the best general chance of surviving varied rooms.

Rival advancement should therefore not be resolved only by comparing combat level against node threat. When abstracting movement, consider whether the team's actual toolkit addresses the node's dominant problem.

## Closing Ring pressure

The Ring compresses geography but should not rewrite a contestant's mechanical identity.

Its important effects are loss of preferred terrain, forced resource expenditure, reduced opportunities to rest, and collision between entries that previously avoided one another. A stealth pair forced onto a broad exposed route has lost part of its effective power. A heavily armored five-person team may actually improve relative to rivals when narrow secret paths close and stable engineered routes become more valuable.

## Current design boundary

This framework does not assign exact starting micronodes or gem colors. Those remain governed by Map Designer topology and the Campaign Architect's constrained gem distribution.

It also does not yet lock final names, ancestries, homelands, subclasses, spell lists, magic items, AC, HP, save DCs, or complete Foundry stat blocks. Those should be built in the next contestant pass after the Project Lead accepts the mechanical spread.

The next NPC Designer pass should turn these ten slots into specific worldwide contestants, beginning with the highest mechanical-impact entries. Each character should be built from mechanics outward: class/stat identity, equipment, abilities, route capability, and survival profile first; personality, history, sponsor relationship, and emergent relationship potential second.

Organisers and sponsors should remain a later layer until this revised contestant field is accepted.
