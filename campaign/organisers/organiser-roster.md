# The Eight Organisers

Status: Canonical organiser roster. The Eight, their core personalities, Favor philosophies, weaknesses, and council relationships are approved canon; numerical Favor implementation remains Systems Designer responsibility.

## Core principle

The Eight belong to the absolute upper stratum of society. They are sovereigns, great nobles, supreme generals, archmages, high religious authorities, legendary adventurers, intelligence powerbrokers, culture-shaping celebrities, merchant-princes, and comparable figures. Their achievements and personal capabilities sit roughly in the conceptual space of level-20 characters, but they do not require literal PC sheets or a rigid one-organiser/one-class mapping.

They are not gods, demigods, or omnipotent custodians of the volcano. Their individual power is extraordinary, but it does not grant them ordinary control over the mountain below the Crucible.

Favor philosophy and adventuring identity are separate dimensions. Each organiser embodies a recognizable philosophy of excellence, while their own power may draw on several martial, arcane, divine, primal, social, clandestine, or occult traditions. Favor represents personal approval, not an alignment score and not a class affinity.

Magic is cross-cutting. The same spell can produce opposite reactions depending on its purpose and execution: overwhelming battle magic can please Arcos, precise control can please Metonius, healing and protection can please Veyra, strange magical discovery can please Nymor, illusion/divination/enchantment can please Sable, extraordinary visible magic can please Valerius, oath- or bargain-magic can please Nadira, and efficient utility magic can please Kessa.

Their boons and hindrances should express personality and station: information, equipment, rare magic, temporary aid, public endorsement, access, interference, withheld assistance, difficult challenges, political pressure, priestly authority, intelligence assets, military resources, or sponsor influence. Exact mechanical values belong to Systems Designer.

The eight Favor philosophies cover conquest, strategy and martial discipline, mercy, discovery, cunning, spectacle, diplomacy, and survival. Their personal power traditions deliberately overlap those categories rather than mapping onto them one-for-one.

## Elite distance and moral compromise

The Eight are cultured, intelligent, sophisticated and extraordinarily capable, but most do not fundamentally regard ordinary contestants as social equals. They can admire, protect, recruit, mourn, study, exploit or even love individual contestants while remaining members of an elite society emotionally accustomed to the Games killing many entrants.

Their relationships to the institution differ. Some delight in it; some defend its tradition or strategic value; some value the knowledge, politics, culture or legitimacy it produces; some dislike or even despise parts of it. Dissent does not make an organiser innocent. Any organiser who remains seated among the Eight helps sustain the institution and accepts a degree of responsibility for what it does.

## High-level identity spectrum

| Organiser | Elite station | Broad power tradition | Personal relationship to the Games |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arcos | Sovereign of Orinth | epic martial conqueror; giant-blooded/rune-inflected battle power and legendary war gear | Enthralled by the Games as visible proof of strength, legitimacy and hierarchy |
| Metonius | Supreme general and elder royal prince | tactical martial master reinforced by command magic, wards, battle-divination and legendary military relics | Values the Gauntlet as a serious proving ground; dislikes waste and corrupted spectacle more than lethality itself |
| Veyra Halden | Transnational high religious authority and war-saint | divine miracle-worker, protector, exorcist and holy warrior | Condemns much of its unnecessary suffering and has fought for reform, yet remains an organiser and therefore helps legitimize the institution she criticizes |
| Nymor Pell | Archmage, celebrated explorer and scholarly authority | high arcana, planar theory, divination, abjuration and dangerous ritual scholarship | Values the Games as unparalleled access to discovery; can become disturbingly willing to treat contestant risk as the cost of knowledge |
| Sable Vey | International intelligence powerbroker | master infiltrator using shapeshifting, enchantment, divination, shadow-work, memory craft and occult true-name methods | Sees the Games as a uniquely pure intelligence ecosystem; protects its integrity more readily than its contestants |
| Valerius Morn | Prime Victor, cultural icon and elite court figure | legendary bard-duelist with glamour, illusion, enchantment and spectacular battle magic | Loves the Games and the culture around them; death can become story to him before it becomes grief |
| Nadira Kes | Merchant-prince, treaty architect and foreign powerbroker | social mastery backed by oath-binding, geas-like compulsion, abjuration, enchantment and contract magic | Values the Games as an international political compact and market of power; morality rarely outweighs institutional utility for her |
| Kessa Reed | Legendary expedition master and primal authority | ranger-druid mastery, beast/terrain magic, weather sense, transformation and austere survival craft | Dislikes much of the Games' heroic death-culture, but remains because she values the survival test and the influence her seat gives her |

These are broad identities, not mandatory character sheets. An organiser may use methods outside the listed tradition, and no kind of magic automatically belongs to one organiser's Favor.
## 1. Arcos — The Crown of War

**Role:** Ruler of the primary kingdom and the most politically powerful organiser.
**Nature:** Goliath; younger brother of Metonius.
**Power model:** Level-20-scale martial sovereign; Fighter/Barbarian-style conqueror augmented by giant-blooded rune craft, legendary war gear, and the kind of supernatural battlefield endurance expected of an epic martial figure.
**Public identity:** The ruler who believes strength is proven by taking what resistance refuses to yield.

Arcos values decisive violence, dominance, courage under impossible pressure, finishing a dangerous enemy, and refusing humiliation. He especially admires contestants who seize initiative and turn fear into aggression.

He does not require mindless slaughter. He can respect a negotiated surrender if it is obvious the victor could have killed everyone and chose terms from a position of strength. What he despises is weakness disguised as virtue.

**Favor rises from:** defeating powerful opponents, accepting dangerous challenges, breaking enemy formations, refusing intimidation, protecting a claim through force, spectacular finishing blows.
**Favor falls from:** pleading when leverage exists, abandoning a winnable fight from fear, letting a dangerous enemy recover without extracting a price, public submission, empty threats.

**Likely boons:** weapons, offensive consumables, temporary combat enhancements, information about strong opponents, opportunities for sanctioned challenges.
**Likely hindrances:** withdrawal of martial aid, deliberately harsher challenge opportunities, public contempt, favoring a rival known for decisive action.

Arcos cares about the Games because they reinforce his worldview: greatness should be visible, tested, and undeniable. He considers the Gauntlet one of the few institutions where birth cannot completely protect the incompetent.
## 2. Metonius — The Old General

**Role:** Supreme general of Arcos' armies and senior military voice among the organisers.
**Nature:** Goliath; older brother of Arcos.
**Power model:** Level-20-scale tactical martial; Battle Master-style commander whose personal mastery is reinforced by command wards, battle-divination, enchanted standards, and military relics rather than conventional spellcasting.
**Public identity:** A commander who values the battle won with the fewest foolish losses more than the loudest victory.

Metonius values positioning, discipline, intelligent retreats, target selection, use of terrain, protection of allies, combined arms, and knowing when an apparently weaker enemy is actually the greater threat.

He never sought the throne. He believes command is a craft, not a birthright, and is more interested in whether people can lead than whether they can rule.

**Favor rises from:** controlling chokepoints, clever formations, rescuing an ally without collapsing the plan, retreating before disaster, defeating stronger opposition through preparation, conserving limited resources.
**Favor falls from:** wasteful charges, abandoning allies for personal glory, ignoring terrain, repeating failed tactics, sacrificing people for spectacle when another option existed.

**Likely boons:** tactical intelligence, maps or enemy tendencies, defensive gear, battlefield consumables, warnings about dangerous rival movements.
**Likely hindrances:** withholding intelligence, backing a more disciplined rival, arranging tests that punish sloppy planning rather than raw weakness.

Metonius cares because the Games reveal command under imperfect information. He has watched rulers, generals, mercenaries, and heroes discover that courage without judgment is only another way to die. He dislikes pointless contestant deaths, but not because he believes the Games should be safe: in his view, a test without real consequences eventually becomes theater. He accepts mortality as part of what makes judgment meaningful.
## 3. Veyra Halden — The Open Hand

**Role:** High religious authority, healer, protector, and moral counterweight within the organiser council.
**Nature:** Human war-saint and veteran of several wars and disaster campaigns.
**Power model:** Level-20-scale Cleric/Paladin-style hierophant: miracle healing, protection, banishment, sanctified combat, and divine authority strong enough to alter the outcome of wars or mass-casualty crises.
**Public identity:** The organiser who believes strength is proven most clearly by what someone refuses to do when cruelty would be easy.

Veyra values rescue, healing, accepting surrender, protecting non-contestants, keeping frightened people alive, and showing restraint after victory. She does not confuse mercy with passivity: she respects contestants who fight brutally when necessary and stop the moment necessity ends.

**Favor rises from:** saving wounded rivals, protecting locals, accepting surrender, sharing scarce healing, risking position to prevent needless death, refusing torture or execution for convenience.
**Favor falls from:** cruelty after victory, killing helpless opponents, exploiting civilians, abandoning someone who could reasonably be saved, using suffering primarily as entertainment.

**Likely boons:** healing resources, condition relief, protective charms, information about injured or endangered contestants, opportunities to save lives that carry wider consequences.
**Likely hindrances:** withdrawal of restorative aid, public condemnation, directing mercy-minded sponsors toward rival teams.

Veyra cares because she has spent a lifetime seeing powerful people call unnecessary suffering inevitable. She considers the Gauntlet a test of whether ambition erases conscience when nobody can force contestants to behave well. She also knows that her presence on the council makes the Games easier for respectable society to defend. She has forced reforms and prevented abuses, but she has not abolished the institution, resigned from it, or paid the political cost that open war against it might demand. That compromise is part of who she is.
## 4. Nymor Pell — The Seeker Beyond the Door

**Role:** Scholar, explorer, and principal advocate for discovery over simple qualification.
**Nature:** Gnome archmage and field archaeologist.
**Power model:** Level-20-scale archmage with strong divination, abjuration, planar scholarship, ritual magic, and expedition utility; his credibility rests on surviving and understanding magical phenomena that kill lesser scholars.
**Public identity:** The organiser most interested in what contestants learn about the mountain that the organisers themselves do not know.

Nymor values curiosity, puzzles, hidden routes, careful study, preserving ancient sites, testing strange mechanisms, and recognizing when knowledge is worth more than immediate position.

He dislikes destructive ignorance more than failure. A team that cannot solve a chamber may still impress him by documenting it intelligently; a team that smashes an irreplaceable mechanism because it was faster may lose him completely.

**Favor rises from:** solving puzzles, discovering new routes, preserving records, learning local history, identifying lost infrastructure, asking good questions, returning with information nobody else possessed.
**Favor falls from:** destroying discoveries unnecessarily, ignoring obvious mysteries, falsifying findings, looting unique knowledge without understanding it, refusing every uncertain route purely from impatience.

**Likely boons:** route fragments, translation assistance, divinatory clues, puzzle tools, protective magic for exploration, access to restricted historical records.
**Likely hindrances:** withholding clues, refusing scholarly assistance, publicly crediting another team for discoveries first.

Nymor cares because the Gauntlet remains one of the few places in the known world where even its hosts can still be surprised. He would consider a Games that revealed nothing new to be a disappointing year. He does not wish contestants dead, but his curiosity can make him alarmingly comfortable with other people accepting risks that produce knowledge he could not obtain safely himself.
## 5. Sable Vey — The Knife Behind the Smile

**Role:** Intelligence master, infiltrator, and judge of deception.
**Nature:** Changeling spymaster whose public face is only one of many identities.
**Power model:** Level-20-scale Rogue/Bard/Warlock-style occult operator: infiltration and social mastery reinforced by shapeshifting, enchantment, divination, memory craft, shadow-working, and true-name methods whose exact sources she does not disclose.
**Public identity:** The organiser who believes winning without allowing an opponent to understand how they lost is a legitimate form of mastery.

Sable values deception, theft, infiltration, traps, misdirection, impersonation, information asymmetry, and creating choices where every answer benefits you.

She does not automatically reward lying. Bad lies, pointless betrayal, or deception that creates no advantage bore her. She admires schemes that survive scrutiny and opponents who recognize when they themselves are being manipulated.

**Favor rises from:** stealing without detection, feeding believable false information, turning rivals against each other, escaping traps through deception, manipulating gem trades, exploiting assumptions, discovering someone else's scheme.
**Favor falls from:** obvious lies, needless betrayal, getting caught through carelessness, using deception where simple competence would have worked better, confusing cruelty with cleverness.

**Likely boons:** intelligence, disguises, infiltration tools, false documents, misleading information about rivals that is accurate in carefully limited ways.
**Likely hindrances:** feeding rivals useful intelligence, withholding warnings, exposing embarrassing secrets, testing whether contestants can recognize planted misinformation.

Sable cares because the Games compress politics, espionage, theft, and survival into a place where consequences are immediate. She regards them as the purest intelligence contest in the world precisely because nobody can rely on institutions to protect them.
## 6. Valerius Morn — The Voice of the Crowd

**Role:** Master of ceremonies, patron of spectacle, and organiser most closely associated with the public face of the Games.
**Nature:** Tiefling bard, duelist, and former arena performer.
**Power model:** Level-20-scale Bard/duelist with exceptional glamour, illusion, enchantment, performance, social command, and enough battle magic to make spectacle physically dangerous rather than merely theatrical.
**Public identity:** The organiser who believes greatness should be memorable, not merely efficient.

Valerius values daring, speeches, reversals, public challenges, dramatic rescues, audacious risks, wit under pressure, and contestants who understand when the audience is watching.

He does not require stupidity for entertainment. A perfectly timed retreat can impress him if it becomes a story. What he dislikes is wasting attention: dull caution under Spotlight, anticlimactic behavior, or taking no position when the moment demands one.

**Favor rises from:** dramatic victories, public vows, bold reversals, stylish use of the environment, memorable negotiations, theatrical defiance, making a Spotlight moment matter.
**Favor falls from:** anticlimax, wasting Spotlight, repetitive behavior, retreating without purpose, refusing every dramatic opportunity, making the Games boring through indecision.

**Likely boons:** audience-driven sponsor attention, performance-oriented magic, public endorsements, information released through the broadcast, opportunities to issue or answer public challenges.
**Likely hindrances:** public ridicule, withdrawing Spotlight opportunities, promoting rivals into more favorable audience narratives, framing a team's failure in ways that damage reputation.

Valerius cares because the Games are the greatest stage in the world. He genuinely believes stories of courage, folly, betrayal, and triumph outlive kingdoms, and he considers forgettable excellence a kind of failure.
## 7. Nadira Kes — The Golden Measure

**Role:** Diplomat, negotiator, and architect of many of the political agreements that sustain the Games outside the arena.
**Nature:** Human stateswoman and merchant-prince.
**Power model:** Level-20-scale Bard/Warlock-style powerbroker whose social, legal, and economic mastery is reinforced by oath-binding, geas-like compulsion, abjuration, enchantment, and contract magic.
**Public identity:** The organiser who believes power is greatest when other people choose to participate in your plan.

Nadira values alliances, gem trades, hostage exchanges without bloodshed, negotiated passage, debt, coalition building, information markets, and agreements that remain useful under pressure.

She cares less about whether a bargain is generous than whether it is intelligent, understood, and honored. She respects hard bargaining. She despises agreements made casually and broken the moment they become inconvenient.

**Favor rises from:** profitable exchanges, coalition building, keeping difficult bargains, negotiating passage, turning duplicate gems into leverage, resolving conflicts without destroying useful relationships, recognizing mutual interest.
**Favor falls from:** breaking agreements without necessity, destroying potential allies for trivial gains, making trades without understanding their cost, refusing negotiation from pride, creating debts that cannot realistically be paid.

**Likely boons:** introductions, sponsor connections, trade opportunities, information markets, temporary access to resources normally reserved for other factions, leverage against another team.
**Likely hindrances:** closing doors with sponsors, strengthening rival coalitions, making unfavorable information public, arranging opportunities where the cost of prior promises becomes unavoidable.

Nadira cares because the Gauntlet is a compressed model of international politics. Gems are scarce, information is asymmetric, violence is expensive, and no team can possess every resource it needs. To her, that makes negotiation as real a weapon as any blade.
## 8. Kessa Reed — The Last Trail

**Role:** Explorer, survivalist, and judge of endurance.
**Nature:** Halfling ranger and veteran of expeditions that killed larger, stronger companions.
**Power model:** Level-20-scale Ranger/Druid-style primal master: legendary fieldcraft joined to terrain magic, beast and plant communion, transformation, weather sense, and the ability to survive environments that function as encounters for ordinary adventurers.
**Public identity:** The organiser who believes the best victory is the one you are still alive to claim.

Kessa values preparation, navigation, resource discipline, camouflage, terrain use, avoiding unwinnable fights, maintaining food and water, choosing safe rests, and knowing when pride is trying to get you killed.

She is unimpressed by reckless heroics unless the risk was necessary. She can admire a team for hiding from something Arcos thinks they should fight, and she is often the organiser most pleased by a clean escape from an Extreme threat.

**Favor rises from:** surviving hostile terrain, conserving resources, recognizing overwhelming danger, successful retreats, clever camps, finding water and safe passage, using monsters or weather rather than confronting them directly.
**Favor falls from:** waste, repeated preventable injuries, ignoring obvious warning signs, exhausting the team for vanity, refusing retreat because an audience is watching, poor preparation.

**Likely boons:** survival gear, environmental protection, warnings about terrain, safe-rest information, navigation aids, consumables that extend endurance.
**Likely hindrances:** withholding practical aid, allowing wasteful teams to suffer the consequences of poor preparation, favoring rivals who use the mountain more intelligently.

Kessa cares because strength is routinely overvalued by people who have never been truly lost, hungry, injured, and hunted. She wants the Games to prove that survival is a form of mastery rather than an absence of courage. She dislikes much of the institution's heroic death-culture, yet she has chosen influence from inside the Eight over principled withdrawal. She knows that decision also makes her complicit in preserving the Games.
## Organiser responsibilities

Their Favor philosophies are personal, but each also has a practical role in maintaining the Games around the organiser-controlled Crucible and public spectacle.

| Organiser | Operational sphere |
|---|---|
| Arcos | Political authority, military backing, legitimacy, high-profile martial challenges |
| Metonius | Security doctrine, contestant threat assessment, tactical review, military logistics |
| Veyra | Medical standards, surrender conventions, survivor treatment, humanitarian disputes |
| Nymor | Archives, recovered lore, magical analysis, route reports, unknown phenomena |
| Sable | Intelligence, anti-cheating investigation, counter-espionage, information security |
| Valerius | Ceremonies, public broadcasts, Spotlight presentation, audience-facing spectacle |
| Nadira | Sponsors, diplomatic agreements, contracts, trade privileges, external political relations |
| Kessa | Insertion readiness, field-survival doctrine, environmental advisories, expedition logistics |

None of these responsibilities grant ordinary control over the volcano itself. Their institutional power is strongest outside the wild arena and at the Crucible.
## Important relationships within the Eight

- **Arcos and Metonius:** Genuine fraternal loyalty with a persistent argument about what strength means. Arcos thinks Metonius can overvalue caution; Metonius thinks Arcos sometimes mistakes momentum for strategy.
- **Arcos and Veyra:** The sharpest philosophical conflict on the council. Arcos suspects mercy often protects weakness; Veyra believes cruelty after victory is the clearest proof that strength has become vanity.
- **Metonius and Kessa:** Strong mutual respect. Both accept retreat and resource preservation when circumstances justify it, though Metonius thinks in terms of campaigns while Kessa thinks in terms of surviving the next mile.
- **Nymor and Kessa:** Frequent allies over exploration, but Kessa is willing to abandon a discovery when staying becomes foolish. Nymor sometimes is not.
- **Sable and Nadira:** Professional rivalry. Sable values leverage obtained without consent; Nadira values leverage converted into voluntary agreement. Each considers the other's method incomplete.
- **Valerius and Arcos:** Often aligned on spectacular confrontation, but Valerius will praise a beautiful failure that Arcos considers worthless.
- **Valerius and Veyra:** Surprisingly compatible when rescues, sacrifices, or acts of restraint become unforgettable public moments; violently opposed when suffering is manufactured purely for entertainment.
- **Nymor and Sable:** Mutual fascination and mistrust. Both prize information, but Nymor wants truth preserved while Sable values controlling who knows it and when.
- **Nadira and Metonius:** Both respect commitments and planning. Nadira prefers solutions where opponents still have incentives afterward; Metonius is more comfortable ending a threat permanently when necessary.

The Eight should disagree openly enough that contestants can learn their values from public commentary, ceremony, prior Games, sponsor gossip, and the consequences of their own actions.
## Same action, different judgments

The roster is strongest when one choice can plausibly move several organisers in different directions.

| Contestant action | Likely reactions |
|---|---|
| Retreats from an obviously overwhelming monster after extracting a wounded ally | Kessa strongly approves; Metonius approves; Veyra approves; Arcos may disapprove unless the withdrawal preserves a larger objective; Valerius depends on how memorable the escape becomes |
| Executes a surrendered rival to secure their gems | Arcos may approve the decisiveness; Veyra strongly disapproves; Metonius asks whether the rival remained a real threat; Nadira dislikes destroying future leverage; Sable cares whether the surrender itself was manipulated |
| Steals a gem through impersonation without fighting | Sable strongly approves; Nadira may approve the leverage but dislike any broken agreement; Metonius respects clean execution; Arcos is difficult to impress unless the theft demonstrates dominance afterward |
| Stops to decipher an ancient mechanism instead of taking the obvious route | Nymor strongly approves; Kessa approves only if the risk and resources remain sensible; Arcos may see wasted momentum; Metonius judges the strategic value of the information obtained |
| Publicly rescues an enemy during Spotlight | Veyra and Valerius may both strongly approve for completely different reasons; Metonius approves if the rescue does not endanger the entire team; Arcos respects it only if it demonstrates confidence rather than weakness |
| Trades duplicate gems to build a temporary coalition | Nadira strongly approves; Metonius may approve the strategic position; Sable looks for hidden leverage; Arcos may dislike dependency unless the team clearly controls the arrangement |

### Magic is judged by intent, not category

No organiser owns "magic Favor." Examples of the same magical traditions producing different judgments:

| Magical action | Possible organiser readings |
|---|---|
| Devastating area magic annihilates a dangerous enemy formation | Arcos may admire overwhelming dominance; Metonius judges whether the target and timing justified the expenditure; Valerius may love the spectacle; Veyra may condemn it if surrender or containment was realistic |
| A control spell ends a battle without casualties | Metonius may strongly approve precision; Veyra may approve restraint; Sable may admire the manipulation of choices; Arcos may respect it only if it clearly demonstrates superiority rather than fear of fighting |
| Healing or protection saves a rival team | Veyra may strongly approve; Metonius may approve if it preserves a useful alliance; Nadira may value the debt created; Arcos may regard it as weakness if no strategic advantage exists |
| Divination reveals a hidden route or rival plan | Nymor values discovery; Sable values information asymmetry; Metonius values actionable intelligence; Kessa values it if it prevents wasteful exposure |
| Illusion or enchantment wins a gem without a fight | Sable may strongly approve; Nadira may admire leverage if agreements remain coherent; Valerius may adore an elegant public deception; Veyra may object if vulnerable people were exploited |
| Minor utility magic solves food, shelter, climbing or navigation efficiently | Kessa may value it highly; Nymor may barely notice unless it reveals something new; Valerius may find it dull unless used inventively under pressure |

The mechanical source of an ability is secondary. What matters to Favor is what the contestant chose to accomplish, at what cost, under what circumstances, and what that choice reveals about them.

## Design boundary

This pass defines personalities, philosophies, rough power identities, council relationships, and thematic boon/hindrance directions.

It does not define numeric Favor thresholds, boon frequency, exact magic items, intervention limits, Spotlight multipliers, or whether an organiser can directly target a team with a mechanical penalty. Those belong to Systems Designer and must preserve the principle that organisers influence the Games without controlling the wild volcano.

The Eight are now sufficiently deep for canonical NPC design. Further organiser detail should be added only when another workstream needs table-facing material, such as Favor events, sponsor relationships, staging scenes, appearances, or runtime voice guidance.
