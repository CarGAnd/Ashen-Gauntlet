# The Ashen Gauntlet: Major Areas of the Volcano

## Purpose

This document establishes the major campaign-scale regions of the Gauntlet, including the jungle and ridge systems surrounding the volcano as well as the great interior regions.
These are not elevation bands and should not be interpreted as a linear sequence from bottom to top.
Most campaign time should be spent moving through, between, above, below, and occasionally back out of these regions.

The Jungle Ring is a full major area in its own right. It contains the starting-position network, early exploration scenes, surface cultures, and many of the first entrances into the mountain. It is broad and internally varied rather than a single homogeneous biome.

Each major area represents a distinct exploration experience, culture, ecology, historical layer, or traversal logic.
Many areas connect to several others, while some remain isolated except for a few difficult or secret entrances.
Some civilizations are dead and archaeological; others are still living, adapting, and politically active.

The volcano web should therefore feel like a collection of partially overlapping worlds rather than a stack of themed floors.

## Major Areas at a Glance

1. The Jungle Ring — the broad exterior network of low jungle, higher jungle, ravines, cliffs, ridges, living cultures, and contestant starting positions.
2. The Buried Kingdom — pre-volcanic settlements entombed beneath ash and stone.
3. The Verdant Expanse — enclosed elven biospheres that became self-sustaining subterranean wildernesses.
4. The Broken Dwarven Realm — the shattered infrastructure and settlements of an ancient industrial civilization.
5. The Veiled Domain — hidden elven sanctums governed by magical geometry rather than ordinary geography.
6. The Labyrinth of Ash — damaged memory architecture that distorts space, time, and perception.
7. The Great Warrens — creature-made tunnels cutting across every historical layer.
8. The Ember Kingdoms — living elemental civilizations in the deep volcanic interior.
9. The Vent-Cities — interconnected kobold settlements threaded through vents, chimneys, ducts, and crawlways.
10. The Shattered Heart — the fault-torn collision zone where ancient systems have been ripped open and intermixed.
11. The Caldera Underworld — the final wild inner region beneath and around the summit before the Crucible.

The Crucible is treated separately because it is the only area truly controlled by the organisers.
## 1. The Jungle Ring

The Jungle Ring is the broad exterior wilderness surrounding and climbing the volcano. It begins in the lower, wetter foreground and rises through higher jungle, ravines, terraces, ash-marked slopes, cliff forests, and broken ridge systems. Jungle vegetation can persist surprisingly high wherever water, soil, shelter, and volcanic warmth allow it.

This region is the primary insertion landscape for the Games. At least twenty-five established contestant starting positions should be distributed across several partially disconnected surface territories rather than along one continuous path. Some starts lie low in river-fed jungle, others on elevated shelves or ridge forests, and apparent altitude should not determine actual progress toward the Crucible.

The Jungle Ring should contain real scenes and meaningful exploration rather than functioning only as a prologue. Its micronodes can include flooded ruins, hunting trails, ravines, waterfalls, tribal settlements, giant-animal territories, cliff paths, abandoned shrines, ash clearings, sinkholes, cave mouths, and fragments of later civilizations exposed at the surface.

Surface territories do not all connect directly to one another. Two teams may start geographically close but be separated by cliffs, rivers, ravines, dense terrain, or incompatible route systems. Conversely, distant surface territories may feed into the same interior network and create later rival-team convergence.

The Jungle Ring should provide multiple punctures into selected major areas, especially the Buried Kingdom, Verdant Expanse, Broken Dwarven Realm, Great Warrens, Vent-Cities, and parts of the Shattered Heart. Deeper or later regions such as the Ember Kingdoms and Caldera Underworld should normally require interior progression first.

Living ridge cultures, including orc clans, ogre families, hill giant households, and other adapted communities, belong naturally here. They know the mountain as a homeland rather than an arena and may control, trade, conceal, or simply use routes that contestants desperately need.

Primary campaign identity: contestant dispersal, broad wilderness exploration, local rival contact, living cultures, apparent route freedom, and discovery of the many ways into the mountain.

## 2. The Buried Kingdom

The Buried Kingdom predates the volcano itself.
When the first great eruptions transformed the landscape, villages, shrines, roads, fields, and settlements were entombed beneath ash, mud, and stone.
Later fissures, burrows, mines, and collapses have reopened fragments of this lost world.

The region should feel like archaeological horror rather than ordinary ruins.
Contestants may walk through streets that have not seen open air for thousands of years, enter homes preserved beneath ash, or discover temple chambers sealed during the first catastrophe.
Ghosts, revenants, wights, specters, and ancestral spirits may remain bound to places whose names have vanished from history.

The Buried Kingdom is not one continuous city.
It is a family of buried settlement pockets connected unpredictably by geology and later intrusions.
Its entrances can therefore appear beneath jungle sinkholes, dwarven excavations, deep warrens, or volcanic fractures.

Primary campaign identity: ancient mystery, death, memory, archaeological horror, and the oldest evidence that the mountain has always been inhabited.

## 3. The Verdant Expanse

The Verdant Expanse consists of immense enclosed biospheres originally engineered by the elves.
Each resembles a vast sealed bowl or terrarium hidden inside the mountain: a self-contained ecological world with its own water cycle, geothermal warmth, magical or mineral light, soil systems, and atmosphere.

Moisture condenses high above, falls as rain, gathers in rivers and lakes, and returns through evaporation and underground circulation.
Dense plant life keeps the interior humid and extraordinarily lush.
Some chambers support giant trees, flowering canopies, fern plains, fungal forests, lakes, marshes, and broad grazing grounds.

The systems are mostly closed.
Only tiny cracks, ventilation channels, root fissures, insect passages, and narrow upper vents connect some bowls to the greater volcano.
Most major entrances were originally deliberate elven access routes or later breaches caused by dwarven excavation, earthquakes, burrowing creatures, or collapsing water systems.

Because each biosphere can have only a handful of meaningful entrances, the Verdant Expanse is both a large internal wilderness and a semi-isolated world.
A team may enter one bowl from deep below and leave through a breach much higher up, while another nearby biosphere may be completely inaccessible without discovering a separate route.

Giant grazers, oversized insects, dinosaurs, giant apes, giant boars, shambling plant creatures, and aerial predators can all occupy these systems.
Flying creatures may nest along the cavern crown, using the tiny high vents as their only connection to the outside world.

Primary campaign identity: enclosed subterranean wilderness, self-sustaining ecology, giant scale, exploration, migration, and hidden elven engineering.
## 4. The Broken Dwarven Realm

The dwarves once established the closest thing the volcano has ever had to a coherent internal civilization.
Their realm included settlements, forge districts, mines, reservoirs, defensive halls, bridges, cargo lifts, roads, aqueducts, and vertical shafts.
The Shattering broke this civilization into disconnected districts that contestants may rediscover repeatedly from different directions.

The Broken Dwarven Realm should function as the great infrastructure labyrinth of the campaign.
It contributes many of the most understandable long-range connections in the spiderweb, but those systems are damaged, blocked, flooded, tilted, occupied, or only partly functional.
Reactivating one ancient mechanism may open access to entirely new parts of the mountain.

Primary campaign identity: monumental engineering, dead civilization, transport infrastructure, industrial ruins, and recoverable connectivity.

## 5. The Veiled Domain

The Veiled Domain is not one city but a dispersed network of hidden elven sanctums, observatories, vaults, laboratories, archives, and ritual complexes.
Its builders followed magical geometry, planar alignments, ley structures, and celestial logic rather than ordinary roads.
As a result, local physical distance is often a poor guide to where its passages lead.

Doors may open onto distant chambers, water may flow in impossible directions, or routes may only become usable after understanding an ancient pattern.
Sphinx vaults, geomantic engines, botanical controls, and preserved archives belong naturally within this layer.

Primary campaign identity: magical mystery, hidden knowledge, non-intuitive navigation, ancient guardianship, and high-value secrets.

## 6. The Labyrinth of Ash

The Labyrinth of Ash began as elven memory architecture: controlled spaces intended to test perception, memory, discipline, or initiation.
Damage from the Shattering and centuries of trapped minds have transformed parts of it into something unstable and dangerous.

Corridors repeat, rooms reappear, memories become physical, and time may pass unevenly.
Previous contestants may still be trapped within, unaware of how much time has elapsed outside.
The Labyrinth can have several unrelated entrances, making it both a deadly trap and a possible shortcut between distant regions.

Primary campaign identity: psychological survival, spatial puzzles, altered perception, trapped history, and unreliable time.

## 7. The Great Warrens

The Great Warrens are the tunnels of enormous burrowing creatures that have crossed the volcano for ages.
They disregard every civilization's intended boundaries and therefore cut directly through buried cities, dwarven works, elven structures, natural caverns, and volcanic strata.

Some tunnels are ancient and abandoned.
Others remain active territory for umber hulks, ankhegs, bulettes, carrion creatures, giant insects, and potentially much larger subterranean predators.
The Warrens are often among the fastest routes through the mountain and among the least predictable.

Primary campaign identity: natural cross-cutting traversal, megafauna, unstable shortcuts, hunting territory, and the sense that something much larger may still be moving below.
## 8. The Ember Kingdoms

The Ember Kingdoms are living societies in the deeper volcanic interior rather than ruins waiting to be explored.
Firenewts, salamanders, azers, magmins, elemental cults, and other heat-adapted peoples may occupy basalt settlements, magma-side temples, ancient forge complexes, and obsidian bridges.

These groups should not form one unified faction.
Some worship fire, some bind it, some exploit it, and some believe the volcano contains a gate or power that must either be opened or protected.
Contestants entering this region become outsiders in functioning societies where diplomacy, trade, trespass, alliance, and religious conflict can matter as much as combat.

Primary campaign identity: living elemental civilization, politics, negotiation, dangerous heat, and deep routes with surprising upward access.

## 9. The Vent-Cities

The Vent-Cities are the distributed kobold civilization threaded through the volcano's vents, chimneys, ducts, cracks, furnace channels, and narrow maintenance spaces.
They are not one city and not one people, but many clans connected through a secondary route network that larger creatures cannot easily use.

Some clans worship living dragons, some revere old draconic relics, some treat furnaces or fire elementals as divine, and others have developed more practical trading relationships with nearby cultures.
A trusted guide can transform an apparently impossible cliff, shaft, or blocked passage into a viable route through the mountain.

Primary campaign identity: vertical social navigation, hidden infrastructure, dragon cults, trade, clan politics, and access unavailable to larger travellers.

## 10. The Shattered Heart

The Shattered Heart is the region where the volcano's major historical systems have been violently broken open and forced together.
Faults slice through dwarven halls, expose elven structures, open buried strata, redirect rivers, and allow lava to flood former roads.
Whole chambers may be tilted, split, suspended, or connected only by unstable remnants of older architecture.

This is the volcano's scar tissue and one of the strongest natural convergence regions in the web.
Routes from otherwise separate worlds can intersect here because the Shattering destroyed the boundaries that once kept them apart.

Primary campaign identity: geological traversal, vertical scale, instability, exposed history, and increasing rival-team convergence.

## 11. The Caldera Underworld

The Caldera Underworld occupies the wild inner mountain beneath and around the summit crater.
It remains outside organiser control despite lying close to the Crucible.
Ash vents, lava shelves, upper dwarven works, collapsed elven mechanisms, dragon territory, fault chambers, and exposed crater passages all overlap here.

By this stage, many distant routes have begun to converge.
The region should still permit meaningful choices, but teams are increasingly likely to encounter one another because fewer viable approaches remain.
Exploration therefore begins to transform into direct competition.

Primary campaign identity: endgame wilderness, route convergence, dragon and volcanic threat, rival pressure, and the final wild approach to the Crucible.

## The Crucible

The Crucible is not another natural macro-region.
It is the organisers' fortified summit arena and the only location they truly control.
Its deliberate architecture, magical spectacle, personnel, and audience-facing infrastructure should contrast sharply with the uncontrolled mountain below.

Reaching it marks the end of the wilderness Gauntlet and the beginning of the qualification and Prime Victor endgame.
